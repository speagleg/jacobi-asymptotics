\section{The hard edge: comparison with a Bessel model}\label{sec:hardedge}
% src: ROUTE2.md §§2 (last paragraph), 3, 4; ROUTE2_xframe1.md §§3-4; ROUTE2_plt1.md §§3.1, 3.3, 4; RH_zeta_close.md §§1.2-1.7.

After Sections~\ref{sec:frame} and~\ref{sec:outer} the orthogonal polynomials of $\sigma_{p,s,\ell_1}$ are encoded in a
matrix $Q$, normalised at infinity, whose jumps are listed in the Frame Proposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}).
Away from the two endpoints $0$ and $4c$ of the band these jumps are those of an explicit global parametrix, up to
exponentially small errors. At the soft edge $4c$ the standard Airy construction applies. This section treats the hard
edge $0$. We compare $Q$ with an explicitly solvable \emph{comparison problem} and show that the two have the same first
two moments at infinity up to $O(1/n)$; Section~\ref{sec:extraction} then reads off $a_n$ and $b_n$.

\emph{Why the usual local parametrix fails.} The band weight is $W_0=C_nx^\alpha(4c-x)H_n(x)r_n(x)$, and the slowly
varying factor enters through $r_n(z)=\ell_1(nz^{-1/p})/\ell_1(n)$. In the logarithmic variable $\xi=\log n-(\log z)/p$
the point $z=0$ corresponds to $\xi=+\infty$, where $\Lambda=\log\ell_1\circ\exp$ has no expansion: $r_n$ is analytic
off $(-\infty,0]$ but it is neither analytic at $0$ nor of Fisher--Hartwig type. The Bessel parametrix of
\ref{tool:T2} needs a function $\tilde W$ with $\tilde W_+\tilde W_-=W_0$ on the band which is continued around $0$
across $(-\delta,0)$. Continuing $r_n^{1/2}$ from the upper and from the lower half-plane produces two values on
$(-\delta,0)$ whose ratio is $\exp\bigl(\frac12(\Lambda(\xi-i\pi/p)-\Lambda(\xi+i\pi/p))\bigr)=1+O(\varepsilon_\Lambda)$,
and for $p\le1$ the continuation even leaves the strip on which $\Lambda$ is assumed to be analytic. The size
$O(\varepsilon_\Lambda)$ tends to $0$ with no rate in $n$. Conjugated by the Bessel model at the scale $|z|\asymp n^{-2}$
it becomes $O(n\varepsilon_\Lambda)$, which is not small, so a quotient (small-norm) argument does not close. A Bessel
order that drifts with $n$ removes only the power-like part $z^{-\Lambda'(\log n)/p}$ of $r_n$ and leaves a remainder
of the same kind. This is the situation of \cite[Remark~4.10]{CD}.

\emph{The remedy, due to Conway and Deift.} We never continue the weight across $(-\delta,0)$. Instead we build the
Bessel parametrix with $\tilde W:=D_W$, the Szeg\H{o} function of the whole band weight. Since $D_W$ is analytic off
$[0,4c]$ and $D_{W,+}D_{W,-}=W_0$, the resulting matrix has exactly the band jump of $Q$ and no jump on $(-\delta,0)$.
It differs from $Q$ only on the lips of the lens, where the true jump contains $D_W^2/W_0$ and the model jump contains
the constant $c_\pm=e^{\mp i\pi\alpha}$. We show that $D_W^2/W_0-c_\pm$ is small in the supremum norm, uniformly on
the lips but without any rate (Lemma~\ref{lem:lip-mismatch}). That is all a Neumann series needs, provided the
resolvent of the comparison problem is bounded uniformly in $n$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:UR-comparison},
Theorem~\ref{thm:UR-true}). The effect on the moments is then computed by the difference formula of Conway and Deift.
The factor $1/n$ comes from \emph{Bessel localisation}: the mismatch carries the factor $e^{2n\varphi}$, which confines it
to $|z|\lesssim n^{-2}$ up to a tail, and there the comparison solution has size $n^{1/2}|n^2z|^{-|\alpha|/2}$; this
turns a mismatch of size $\varepsilon'_n=o(1)$ into an error $O(\varepsilon'_n/n)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}).

The comparison argument of this section is that of Conway and Deift \cite[Theorem~4.7 and Proposition~4.9]{CD}. What we
add is a comparison model built from the Szeg\H{o} function of the full weight, a uniform resolvent bound for a Bessel
edge of any order $|\alpha|<1$, and the treatment of an arbitrary slowly varying factor. Two further differences are
of a technical nature. Our comparison problem is not a second orthogonality problem but an explicit matrix: the global
parametrix together with the two local parametrices. The matching errors on the two circles are then simply part of
the comparison, which spares a separate small-norm argument and gives the $O(1/n)$ terms as explicit integrals. And the
restriction $|\alpha|<1$ is used in exactly three places: a power-weighted Cauchy bound with exponents $\pm\alpha/2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:A-power}); the integrability of $|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}$ on a ray (Hilbert--Schmidt bounds in
Appendix~\ref{app:resolvent}, and Bessel localisation in Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}); and the square integrability
of the comparison solution, which is of size $|z|^{-|\alpha|/2}$ at $0$, on the contour, which the $L^2$ framework of
Conway and Deift requires. This is consistent with the remark of Deift and Piorkowski \cite[\S1]{DP} that at
exponent $1$ the corresponding step ``requires a different proof''.

The section is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:he-conv} fixes conventions and lists what is used from
Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}. Section~\ref{sec:he-model} derives the local form of the Bessel model
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel-local}). Section~\ref{sec:he-comparison} builds the comparison parametrix
(Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison-parametrix}) and the comparison problem. Section~\ref{sec:he-mismatch} proves the lip
mismatch estimate (Lemma~\ref{lem:lip-mismatch}). Section~\ref{sec:he-resolvent} states the two resolvent bounds, and
Section~\ref{sec:he-moments} the effect of the mismatch on the moments (Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}).

\subsection{Conventions and input from the frame}\label{sec:he-conv}

Throughout this section $p>0$, $1<s<1+2p$, $\alpha=(s-1-p)/p\in(-1,1)$, and $\ell_1=e^{\Lambda(\log\cdot)}$ satisfies
\Ath{\vartheta_p}. All matrices are $2\times2$, $|\cdot|$ is a fixed matrix norm, $e_1,e_2$ are the standard basis
vectors and
\[
  J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad M=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\i&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  c_\pm:=e^{\mp i\pi\alpha}.
\]
Constants $C$ may depend on $p$, $s$, $\ell_1$ and on the radii $\delta,\varepsilon$ below, but never on $n$ or on the
point $z$; they may change from line to line. Exponentially small factors are written $e^{-n/C}$. Powers and logarithms
are principal unless said otherwise. The Cauchy transform $\Cauchy$, its boundary values $\Cauchy_\pm$, the operator
$\Cauchy_v f=\Cauchy_-(f(v-I))$ and the class $I+\dC(L^2)$ are as in \ref{tool:T3}.

We work in the frame of $Q:=D_\infty^{-\sigma_3}VD_W^{\sigma_3}$. A matrix $X$ in the frame of $V$ corresponds to
$D_\infty^{-\sigma_3}XD_W^{\sigma_3}$; in particular the global parametrix $P^\infty=D_\infty^{\sigma_3}\Pinfz
D_W^{-\sigma_3}$ becomes $\Pinfz$, which does not involve the weight. We use the following items of
Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}, for $n\ge n_0$ and fixed radii $\delta,\varepsilon>0$ which we may take as small as we
wish.

\begin{itemize}
\item \Fi{1} The contour $\Sigma$ of $Q$ is a finite union of $C^2$ arcs, oriented, with the band $(0,4c)$ oriented from
  left to right and the lips of the lens in $U(0,\delta)$ oriented away from $0$. $Q\to I$ at infinity and
  $Q_+=Q_-v$ on $\Sigma$, with $v^{\pm1}$ bounded uniformly in $n$. Inside $U(0,\delta)$ the contour consists of the
  band and two lips, with
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:he-v}
    v=J\ \text{on }(0,\delta),\qquad v=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ (D_W^2/W_0)e^{2n\varphi}&1\end{pmatrix}+E\ \text{on the lips},
  \end{equation}
  where $W_0$ on a lip is the analytic continuation of $W_0$ from the band and $E$ collects the super-small entries of
  \Fi{4}; there is no jump on $(-\delta,0)$ (and, for $p<1$, none on the seams). The part $\Sigma\cap(U(0,2\delta)\cup U(4c,2\varepsilon))$ does not depend on $n$. The arc length of $\Sigma$ is
  $O(n^p)$, and $\Sigma$ is a Carleson curve with constants independent of $n$: $|\Sigma\cap U(z,\rho)|\le C\rho$ for all
  $z,\rho$, and $\|\Cauchy_\pm\|_{L^2(\Sigma)\to L^2(\Sigma)}\le C$.
\item \Fi{2} $W_0=C_nx^\alpha(4c-x)H_n(x)r_n(x)$, where $r_n(z)=\exp\bigl(\Lambda(\log n-\frac1p\log
  z)-\Lambda(\log n)\bigr)$, $H_n>0$ is analytic on $(0,4c+\varepsilon)$, and $\log H_n-\log H_n(0^+)$ is H\"older
  of exponent $\nu_p$ at $0$, uniformly in $n$, in the sense of \eqref{eq:he-holder} below. The constant $C_n$ cancels
  in $D_W^2/W_0$. $D_W$ is the Szeg\H{o} function of $W_0$ on $[0,4c]$:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:he-szego}
    D_W(z)=\exp\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal R(z)}{2\pi}\int_0^{4c}\frac{\log W_0(x)}{\sqrt{x(4c-x)}}\frac{\diff x}{z-x}\Bigr),
    \qquad \mathcal R(z):=\bigl(z(z-4c)\bigr)^{1/2}\sim z\ (z\to\infty).
  \end{equation}
\item \Fi{3} In $U(0,\delta)$ the lips are the preimages under $\zeta=n^2f(z)$ of the rays
  $\arg\zeta=\mp(\pi-\varthetalip)$ (the upper lip is mapped to the lower ray), and they leave $0$ at the angles
  $\pm\varthetalip$; here $\varthetalip=\pi/3$ for $p\ge1$ and $\varthetalip=\pi p/4$ for $p<1$
  (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}).
\item \Fi{4} The entries collected in $E$ satisfy $|E(z)|\le C\exp(-n|z|^{-1/p}/C)\,|z|^{-C}$ on the lips in
  $U(0,\delta)$.
\item \Fi{5} The global parametrix is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:he-Pinf}
    \Pinfz(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{a(z)+a(z)^{-1}}2&\frac{a(z)-a(z)^{-1}}{2i}\\[1mm]
    -\frac{a(z)-a(z)^{-1}}{2i}&\frac{a(z)+a(z)^{-1}}2\end{pmatrix},\qquad a(z)=\Bigl(\frac{z-4c}{z}\Bigr)^{1/4};
  \end{equation}
  it is analytic in $\C\setminus[0,4c]$, $\det\Pinfz=1$, $P^\infty_{0,+}=P^\infty_{0,-}J$ on $(0,4c)$, and $|\Pinfz(z)|\le
  C|z|^{-1/4}|z-4c|^{-1/4}(1+|z|)^{1/2}$. On $\Sigma\setminus\bigl(U(0,\delta)\cup U(4c,\varepsilon)\cup[0,4c]\bigr)$ one
  has $|v-I|\le Ce^{-n/C}$. In $U(4c,\varepsilon)$ there is an Airy parametrix, which in the frame of $Q$ we write
  $\Phat_r=D_\infty^{-\sigma_3}P_rD_W^{\sigma_3}$: it has exactly the jumps $v$ of $Q$ in $U(4c,\varepsilon)$,
  $\det\Phat_r=1$, $\Phat_r(\Pinfz)^{-1}=I+O(1/n)$ uniformly on $\partial U(4c,\varepsilon)$, and it has the two-scale
  structure \eqref{eq:B-airy-structure} of Appendix~\ref{app:resolvent}.
\item \Fi{6} $f$ is conformal on a neighbourhood of $\overline{U(0,\delta)}$, independent of $n$ and of the weight, real
  on $(-\delta,\delta)$, with $f(0)=0$, $f'(0)<0$, and $f=(\varphi\pm i\pi)^2/4$ in $U(0,\delta)\cap\C_\pm$; moreover
  $\Re\varphi<0$ on the lips.
\item \Fi{7} $Q_\pm\in I+\dC(L^2(\Sigma))$; for fixed $n$, $|Q(z)|\le C_n(1+|z|^{-|\alpha|/2-\eta})$ off $\Sigma$ for
  every small $\eta>0$.
\item \Fi{8} The extraction identities $(Q_1)_{12}(Q_1)_{21}=n^{2p}b_n^2$ and $\mathsf a(Q)=n^pa_n$, which rest on
  Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}.
\end{itemize}
Since $f'(0)<0$ and $f$ is real on $(-\delta,\delta)$, $f$ maps $U(0,\delta)\cap\C_\pm$ into $\C_\mp$, the band
$(0,\delta)$ into $(-\infty,0)$ and $(-\delta,0)$ into $(0,\infty)$; and $|f(z)|\ge|z|/C$, $|f'|+|f'|^{-1}\le C$ on
$U(0,\delta)$. Shrinking $\delta$ if necessary, we may and do assume that on the lips in $U(0,\delta)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-lipangle}
  \tfrac34\varthetalip\le|\arg z|\le\tfrac54\varthetalip .
\end{equation}
Finally put $\nu_p:=\min(1/p,\frac12)$, $T_n:=\log n-\frac1p\log\max(4c,\delta)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-epsprime}
  \varepsilon'_n:=n^{-2\nu_p}\log n+\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)\ \longrightarrow\ 0 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Bessel model and its local form}\label{sec:he-model}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.2 (local form, general alpha incl. alpha = 0); ROUTE2_plt1.md §3.1 (sector pi - theta_L);
% REVIEW_Plan48 checks 7 and F5 (det via Wronskian).

Let $\Psi_\alpha$ be the Bessel model of \ref{tool:T2}, built on the three rays $\arg\zeta=\pm(\pi-\varthetalip)$ and
$\arg\zeta=\pi$ (angle freedom, \ref{tool:T2}(b); for $p\ge1$ these are the rays of \cite[\S6]{KMVV}). We orient them
as in \ref{tool:T2}(a): all three rays point towards the origin, with the $+$ side on the left. Thus $\Psi_\alpha$ is given by (6.23) of \ref{tool:T2} in the sector
$|\arg\zeta|<\pi-\varthetalip$ and by the Hankel formulas in the two thin sectors, it has the jumps (6.16)--(6.18) on the
three rays, and it satisfies the large-$\zeta$ expansion (6.28). We have $\det\Psi_\alpha\equiv1$: in the central
sector this is the Wronskian identity of \ref{tool:T4}, and the jump matrices have determinant $1$.

In the central sector $|\arg2\zeta^{1/2}|<\pi/2$, so all Bessel functions there are principal branches
(\ref{tool:T4}, \S10.25(ii)); and (6.28) remains valid for the rays at $\pm(\pi-\varthetalip)$, since the Bessel and
Hankel functions in (6.23)--(6.25) are then evaluated in closed subsectors of the sectors of validity of the
large-argument expansions \ref{tool:T4}, (10.40.1)--(10.40.5); for instance $|\arg2\zeta^{1/2}|\le\frac12(\pi-\varthetalip)
<\frac12\pi$ in the central sector.

\begin{lemma}[local form of the Bessel model]\label{lem:bessel-local}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.2; RH_zeta_close.md §1.2 "Local form" (alpha = -1/2 case).
Let $\alpha\in(-1,1)$ and let $\mathcal S_\pm$ denote the thin sectors $\pi-\varthetalip<\pm\arg\zeta<\pi$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\alpha\ne0$, there is an entire matrix function $\hat\Psi_\alpha$ with $\det\hat\Psi_\alpha=-2i\sin\pi\alpha$
  such that
  \[
    \Psi_\alpha(\zeta)=\hat\Psi_\alpha(\zeta)\,\zeta^{\alpha\sigma_3/2}\,\mathsf S,\qquad
    \mathsf S:=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\\[1mm]0&\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  in $|\arg\zeta|<\pi-\varthetalip$, while in $\mathcal S_\pm$ the constant $\mathsf S$ is replaced by $\mathsf S$ times
  a constant jump matrix of $\Psi_\alpha$.
\item[(ii)] If $\alpha=0$, there is an entire $\hat\Psi_0$ with $\det\hat\Psi_0=1$ such that
  \[
    \Psi_0(\zeta)=\hat\Psi_0(\zeta)\begin{pmatrix}1&\kappa\log\zeta\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \kappa=-\frac{i}{2\pi},
  \]
  in $|\arg\zeta|<\pi-\varthetalip$, while in $\mathcal S_\pm$ the right-hand side is multiplied on the right by a
  constant jump matrix.
\item[(iii)] Consequently, for $0<|\zeta|\le3$ off the rays,
  \[
    |\Psi_\alpha(\zeta)^{\pm1}|\le C|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2},\qquad
    |\Psi_\alpha(\zeta)e_2e_1^{\mathsf T}\Psi_\alpha(\zeta)^{-1}|\le C|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}\qquad(\alpha\ne0),
  \]
  and the same with $|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2}$, $|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}$ replaced by $1+|\log|\zeta||$ and
  $(1+|\log|\zeta||)^2$ if $\alpha=0$. The same bounds hold for the boundary values on the rays.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We expand the entries of (6.23) by the power series \ref{tool:T4}(10.25.2) and read off the singular factors.

(i) Let $\nu=\pm\alpha$. By (10.25.2) with $x=2\zeta^{1/2}$ we have $(\frac12x)^{\nu+2k}=\zeta^{\nu/2+k}$, hence
\[
  I_\nu(2\zeta^{1/2})=\zeta^{\nu/2}E_\nu(\zeta),\qquad
  2\pi i\zeta^{1/2}I_\nu'(2\zeta^{1/2})=\pi i\zeta^{\nu/2}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\nu+2k)\zeta^k}{k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}
  =:\zeta^{\nu/2}F_\nu(\zeta),
\]
with $E_\nu(\zeta)=\sum_k\zeta^k/(k!\Gamma(\nu+k+1))$; $E_\nu$ and $F_\nu$ are entire because $1/\Gamma$ is entire.
Write $g_\nu:=(E_\nu,F_\nu)^{\mathsf T}$, so that the first column of (6.23) is $\zeta^{\alpha/2}g_\alpha$. By (10.27.4),
$\frac i\pi K_\alpha=\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}(I_{-\alpha}-I_\alpha)$, and differentiating,
$-2\zeta^{1/2}K_\alpha'(2\zeta^{1/2})=\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\cdot2\pi i\zeta^{1/2}(I'_{-\alpha}-I'_\alpha)(2\zeta^{1/2})$
(both sides equal $-\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi\alpha}\zeta^{1/2}(I'_{-\alpha}-I'_\alpha)$). So the second column of (6.23) is
$\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}(\zeta^{-\alpha/2}g_{-\alpha}-\zeta^{\alpha/2}g_\alpha)$, and
\[
  \Psi_\alpha=\bigl(g_\alpha\ \ g_{-\alpha}\bigr)\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{\alpha/2}&-\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\zeta^{\alpha/2}\\
  0&\frac{i}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\zeta^{-\alpha/2}\end{pmatrix}=\hat\Psi_\alpha\,\zeta^{\alpha\sigma_3/2}\mathsf S,\qquad
  \hat\Psi_\alpha:=\bigl(g_\alpha\ \ g_{-\alpha}\bigr).
\]
Since $\det\Psi_\alpha=1$ and $\det\mathsf S=i/(2\sin\pi\alpha)$, $\det\hat\Psi_\alpha=-2i\sin\pi\alpha\ne0$. In a thin
sector, $\Psi_\alpha$ is the continuation of the central formula multiplied on the right by a constant jump matrix,
because the jumps of $\Psi_\alpha$ are constant and $\hat\Psi_\alpha\zeta^{\alpha\sigma_3/2}\mathsf S$ is analytic in
$\C\setminus(-\infty,0]$.

(ii) By (10.31.2), $K_0(x)=-(\ln\frac x2+\gamma)I_0(x)+S_0(\frac14x^2)$ with $S_0$ entire. At $x=2\zeta^{1/2}$,
$\ln\frac x2=\frac12\log\zeta$, so $\frac i\pi K_0(2\zeta^{1/2})=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\log\zeta\cdot E_0(\zeta)+\text{(entire)}$.
Differentiating the same identity, $-2\zeta^{1/2}K_0'(2\zeta^{1/2})=I_0(2\zeta^{1/2})+(\frac12\log\zeta+\gamma)\,
2\zeta^{1/2}I_0'(2\zeta^{1/2})-2\zeta S_0'(\zeta)$, and $2\zeta^{1/2}I_0'(2\zeta^{1/2})=F_0(\zeta)/(\pi i)$. Hence the
second column equals $\kappa\log\zeta\cdot g_0(\zeta)+h(\zeta)$ with $\kappa=-i/(2\pi)$ and $h$ entire, and
$\Psi_0=(g_0\ \ h)\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\kappa\log\zeta\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$; $\det(g_0\ h)=\det\Psi_0=1$.
The thin sectors are treated as in (i).

(iii) On $|\zeta|\le3$ the entire factors and their inverses are bounded, and the constant matrices are bounded. The
middle factors satisfy $|\zeta^{\pm\alpha\sigma_3/2}|\le C|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2}$, respectively
$|\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\kappa\log\zeta\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)^{\pm1}|\le C(1+|\log|\zeta||)$. For the
rank-one product write
\[
  \Psi_\alpha e_2e_1^{\mathsf T}\Psi_\alpha^{-1}=\hat\Psi_\alpha\,\zeta^{\alpha\sigma_3/2}
  (\mathsf S_je_2)(e_1^{\mathsf T}\mathsf S_j^{-1})\zeta^{-\alpha\sigma_3/2}\,\hat\Psi_\alpha^{-1};
\]
the entries of the middle matrix are constants times $1,\zeta^{\alpha},\zeta^{-\alpha}$, whence the bound $C|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}$; for $\alpha=0$ the
same computation gives entries of size $(1+|\log|\zeta||)^2$.
\end{proof}

\emph{The model in the normalisation used below.} Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-Phi}
  \Phi_\alpha(\zeta):=\sigma_3\Psi_\alpha(\zeta)\sigma_3\,e^{-2\zeta^{1/2}\sigma_3}.
\end{equation}
This is the left-endpoint model of \ref{tool:T2}(c) with the exponential factor attached; it does not depend on $n$.
Orient the three rays $\Gamma_\alpha:=(-\infty,0]\cup\{\arg\zeta=\pm(\pi-\varthetalip)\}$ \emph{away} from the origin.
Reversing the orientation of a ray interchanges its $\pm$ sides, and $\sigma_3J^{-1}\sigma_3=J$,
$\sigma_3\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\x&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)^{-1}\sigma_3=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\x&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
and $e^{2w\sigma_3}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\x&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)e^{-2w\sigma_3}
=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\xe^{-4w}&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. With (6.16)--(6.18) this gives:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-Phi-jumps}
  \Phi_{\alpha,+}=\Phi_{\alpha,-}J\ \text{on }(-\infty,0),\qquad
  \Phi_{\alpha,+}=\Phi_{\alpha,-}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\e^{\mp i\pi\alpha}e^{-4\zeta^{1/2}}&1\end{pmatrix}\ \text{on }
  \arg\zeta=\mp(\pi-\varthetalip).
\end{equation}
On $(-\infty,0)$ we used that the two boundary values $w_\pm$ of $\zeta^{1/2}$ satisfy $w_+=-w_-$, so that
$e^{2w_-\sigma_3}Je^{-2w_+\sigma_3}=J$. Moreover $\det\Phi_\alpha=1$, and $\Phi_\alpha$ has the two-scale structure
\begin{align}
  \Phi_\alpha(\zeta)&=\zeta^{-\sigma_3/4}\Theta_\infty(\zeta),\quad
  \Theta_\infty=(2\pi)^{-\sigma_3/2}\bigl(M^{-1}+O(\zeta^{-1/2})\bigr),\quad |\Theta_\infty^{\pm1}|\le C
  &&(|\zeta|\ge2),\label{eq:he-Phi-large}\\
  \Phi_\alpha(\zeta)&=\hat A(\zeta)\,\mathcal P(\zeta)\,\Theta_0(\zeta),\quad |\Theta_0^{\pm1}|\le C
  &&(|\zeta|\le3),\label{eq:he-Phi-small}
\end{align}
where $\hat A=\sigma_3\hat\Psi_\alpha\sigma_3$ is entire with entire inverse, $\Theta_0=\sigma_3\mathsf S_j\sigma_3
e^{-2\zeta^{1/2}\sigma_3}$ in the $j$-th sector, and $\mathcal P(\zeta)=\zeta^{\alpha\sigma_3/2}$ for $\alpha\ne0$,
$\mathcal P(\zeta)=I-\kappa(\log\zeta)e_1e_2^{\mathsf T}$ for $\alpha=0$. Indeed \eqref{eq:he-Phi-large} follows from
(6.28), since $\sigma_3M\sigma_3=M^{-1}$ and the exponential factors cancel; the bound on $\Theta_\infty^{-1}$ holds for
large $|\zeta|$ by the expansion and for $2\le|\zeta|\le R$ because $\det\Theta_\infty=1$ and $\Theta_\infty$ is
bounded. \eqref{eq:he-Phi-small} is Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel-local} conjugated by $\sigma_3$.

\subsection{The comparison parametrix and the comparison problem}\label{sec:he-comparison}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.1 Lemma H1'; RH_zeta_close.md §1.2 Lemma H1; ROUTE2_plt1.md §3.1; ROUTE2_xframe1.md §2 (row "§4.1").

We first relate $e^{n\varphi}$ to the Bessel variable $\zeta=n^2f(z)$. In $U(0,\delta)\cap\C_+$ both $(\varphi+i\pi)/2$ and
the principal root $f^{1/2}$ are analytic (the latter because $f$ avoids $(-\infty,0]$ there), and their squares agree
by \Fi{6}. So $(\varphi+i\pi)/2=\pm f^{1/2}$ with a fixed sign. On the upper lip $\Re\varphi<0$ while $\Re f^{1/2}>0$, so the
sign is $-$. The lower half-disc is treated in the same way, and we obtain, with $\zeta^{1/2}=nf^{1/2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-ephi}
  e^{n\varphi(z)}=(-1)^ne^{-2\zeta^{1/2}},\qquad e^{2n\varphi(z)}=e^{-4\zeta^{1/2}}\qquad(z\in U(0,\delta)\setminus[0,\delta)).
\end{equation}
On the lips $\arg\zeta=\mp(\pi-\varthetalip)$, so $\Re\zeta^{1/2}=|\zeta|^{1/2}\sin(\varthetalip/2)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-localisation}
  |e^{2n\varphi(z)}|=e^{-4\sin(\varthetalip/2)|\zeta|^{1/2}}\le e^{-n|z|^{1/2}/C}\qquad\text{on the lips in }U(0,\delta).
\end{equation}

Following \ref{tool:T2}(c) with $\tilde W$ replaced by $D_W$, define in $U(0,\delta)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-P0}
  \tilde P_0:=(-1)^nE_n\,\sigma_3\Psi_\alpha(n^2f)\sigma_3\,D_W^{-\sigma_3}e^{n\varphi\sigma_3},\qquad
  E_n:=D_\infty^{\sigma_3}\Pinfz Mf^{\sigma_3/4}(2\pi n)^{\sigma_3/2}.
\end{equation}
In the frame of $Q$ this becomes, by \eqref{eq:he-ephi},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-P0hat}
  \Phat_0:=D_\infty^{-\sigma_3}\tilde P_0D_W^{\sigma_3}=\Ehat\,\Phi_\alpha(n^2f),\qquad
  \Ehat:=\Pinfz Mf^{\sigma_3/4}(2\pi n)^{\sigma_3/2}=A_0\,(2\pi n)^{\sigma_3/2},
\end{equation}
where $A_0:=\Pinfz Mf^{\sigma_3/4}$. Note that $\Phat_0$ contains neither $D_W$ nor any other trace of the weight.

\begin{lemma}[the comparison parametrix]\label{lem:comparison-parametrix}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.1 Lemma H1' (i)-(iii); RH_zeta_close.md §1.2 Lemma H1.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $A_0$ extends to an analytic function on $U(0,\delta)$ with $\det A_0=1$. It depends neither on $n$ nor on the
  weight, and $|A_0^{\pm1}|\le C$ there. In particular $E_n$ is analytic in $U(0,\delta)$, $\det E_n=1$, and
  $D_\infty^{-\sigma_3}E_n(2\pi n)^{-\sigma_3/2}=A_0$.
\item[(ii)] $\Phat_0$ is analytic in $U(0,\delta)\setminus\Sigma$, $\det\Phat_0=1$, and
  \[
    \Phat_{0,+}=\Phat_{0,-}J\ \text{on }(0,\delta),\qquad
    \Phat_{0,+}=\Phat_{0,-}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\c_\pm e^{2n\varphi}&1\end{pmatrix}\ \text{on the lips in }U(0,\delta)\cap\C_\pm;
  \]
  it has no jump on $(-\delta,0)$. Equivalently, $\tilde P_0$ has the band jump of $V$, no jump on $(-\delta,0)$ (nor on
  the seams if $p<1$), and the lip jump $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\c_\pm e^{2n\varphi}/D_W^2&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
\item[(iii)] Uniformly for $\frac12\delta\le|z|\le\delta$ off $\Sigma$, $\Phat_0(z)\Pinfz(z)^{-1}=I+O(1/n)$, and
  $|\Phat_0^{\pm1}(z)|\le C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) On $(0,\delta)$, $P^\infty_{0,+}=P^\infty_{0,-}J$, and since $f$ is negative there and maps $\C_+$ to $\C_-$,
$f_+^{\sigma_3/4}=f_-^{\sigma_3/4}\mathrm{diag}(-i,i)$. A direct computation gives $M^{-1}JM=\mathrm{diag}(i,-i)$, so
$A_{0,+}=P^\infty_{0,-}JMf_-^{\sigma_3/4}\mathrm{diag}(-i,i)=P^\infty_{0,-}M\,\mathrm{diag}(i,-i)\mathrm{diag}(-i,i)f_-^{\sigma_3/4}
=A_{0,-}$. On $(-\delta,0)$ both $\Pinfz$ and $f^{1/4}$ are analytic. So $A_0$ is analytic in $U(0,\delta)\setminus\{0\}$.
At $0$, $|\Pinfz|\le C|z|^{-1/4}$ by \Fi{5} and $|f^{\pm1/4}|\le C|z|^{\pm1/4}$, so $|A_0(z)|\le C|z|^{-1/2}$; an isolated
singularity with this growth is removable. This is the removability argument of \ref{tool:T2}(c), applied to a matrix
that involves only $\Pinfz$, $M$ and $f$, and therefore neither $n$ nor the weight; in particular the hypothesis of
\cite{KMVV} that the weight be analytic at the endpoint plays no role. $\det A_0=1$ since each factor has determinant
$1$, and $A_0^{-1}$ is the adjugate of $A_0$. Shrinking $\delta$ slightly, $A_0^{\pm1}$ are bounded on $U(0,\delta)$.

(ii) $\Ehat$ is analytic, so the jumps of $\Phat_0$ are those of $\zeta\mapsto\Phi_\alpha(\zeta)$ at $\zeta=n^2f(z)$. The
map $z\mapsto n^2f(z)$ is conformal, hence preserves the sides of an oriented curve; it maps the band (left to right)
onto part of $(-\infty,0)$ traversed away from $0$, the lips (away from $0$) onto the rays at $\mp(\pi-\varthetalip)$
traversed away from $0$, and $(-\delta,0)$ into $(0,\infty)$, where $\Phi_\alpha$ is analytic. So
\eqref{eq:he-Phi-jumps} and \eqref{eq:he-ephi} give the stated jumps. For $\tilde P_0=D_\infty^{\sigma_3}\Phat_0
D_W^{-\sigma_3}$, conjugation by the diagonal matrix $D_W^{-\sigma_3}$ turns $J$ into the band jump of $V$ because
$D_{W,+}D_{W,-}=W_0$, turns the lip jump into the stated one, and creates no jump on $(-\delta,0)$ because $D_W$ is
analytic off $[0,4c]$.

(iii) For $\frac12\delta\le|z|\le\delta$ we have $|\zeta|=n^2|f(z)|\ge n^2\delta/C\ge2$. By \eqref{eq:he-Phi-large} and
$(n^2f)^{-\sigma_3/4}=n^{-\sigma_3/2}f^{-\sigma_3/4}$,
\begin{align*}
  \Phat_0&=A_0(2\pi n)^{\sigma_3/2}(n^2f)^{-\sigma_3/4}(2\pi)^{-\sigma_3/2}\bigl(M^{-1}+O(n^{-1})\bigr)\\
  &=\Pinfz Mf^{\sigma_3/4}f^{-\sigma_3/4}\bigl(M^{-1}+O(n^{-1})\bigr)=\Pinfz\bigl(I+O(n^{-1})\bigr),
\end{align*}
and $\Pinfz$, $(\Pinfz)^{-1}$ are bounded on the annulus. Both claims follow.
\end{proof}

\emph{The comparison problem.} Let $\Sigcirc:=\partial U(0,\delta)\cup\partial U(4c,\varepsilon)$, with both circles
oriented clockwise (so that their $+$ side is the outside), and $\Sighat:=\Sigma\cup\Sigcirc$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-Qtilde}
  \tilde Q(z):=\begin{cases}\Phat_0(z),& z\in U(0,\delta),\\ \Phat_r(z),& z\in U(4c,\varepsilon),\\
  \Pinfz(z),& \text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad z\notin\Sighat .
\end{equation}
Then $\tilde Q$ is analytic off $\Sighat$, $\det\tilde Q=1$, $\tilde Q\to I$ at infinity, and
$\tilde Q_+=\tilde Q_-\tilde v$ on $\Sighat$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-vtilde}
  \tilde v=\begin{cases}J & \text{on }(0,4c),\\
  \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\c_\pm e^{2n\varphi}&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr) & \text{on the lips in }U(0,\delta)\cap\C_\pm,\\
  v & \text{on }\Sigma\cap U(4c,\varepsilon),\\
  I & \text{on }\Sigma\setminus\bigl(U(0,\delta)\cup U(4c,\varepsilon)\cup[0,4c]\bigr),\\
  \tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\tilde Q_{\mathrm{out}} & \text{on }\Sigcirc,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison-parametrix}(ii), \Fi{5}, and the fact that $\Pinfz$ has only the band jump $J$. Here
$\tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}$, $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{out}}$ are the values from inside and outside the circle. We extend $v$ by
$I$ to $\Sigcirc$; $Q$ has no jump there. We call the problem ``$\tilde Q_+=\tilde Q_-\tilde v$ on $\Sighat$,
$\tilde Q\to I$'' the \emph{comparison problem}; it is solved explicitly by \eqref{eq:he-Qtilde}.

The two problems differ in three places. On the circles, $v-\tilde v=I-\tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\tilde
Q_{\mathrm{out}}=O(1/n)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison-parametrix}(iii) and \Fi{5}. On
$\Sigma\setminus(U(0,\delta)\cup U(4c,\varepsilon)\cup[0,4c])$, $|v-\tilde v|\le Ce^{-n/C}$ by \Fi{5}. On the lips in
$U(0,\delta)$, $v-\tilde v=d_ne_2e_1^{\mathsf T}+E$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-dn}
  d_n(z):=\Bigl(\frac{D_W(z)^2}{W_0(z)}-c_\pm\Bigr)e^{2n\varphi(z)} .
\end{equation}
This last difference is the subject of the next subsection.

\subsection{The lip mismatch}\label{sec:he-mismatch}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.3 Lemma LW1'; ROUTE2_xframe1.md §3 (p = 1); ROUTE2_plt1.md §3.3 (p < 1, incl. REVIEW_Plan51 F5);
% RH_zeta_close.md §1.3 (precedent).

The H\"older hypothesis on $H_n$ from \Fi{2} is used in the following form: for $z$ in the closed sectors
$\{0<|z|\le\delta,\ |\arg z|\le\frac54\varthetalip\}$ (to which $H_n$ continues analytically from the band, from each
side) and for $x\in(0,4c]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-holder}
  |\log H_n(z)-\log H_n(0^+)|\le C|z|^{\nu_p},\qquad |\log H_n(x)-\log H_n(0^+)|\le C,
\end{equation}
uniformly in $n$. (For $p>1$ this is the H\"older endpoint bound for the Binet-cut and Stirling factors; for $p=1$
$\log H_n$ is smooth at $0$; for $p<1$ it follows from the corner bound for the seam scalar, which gives
$C|z|(1+\log(1/|z|))\le C|z|^{1/2}$.)

\begin{lemma}[lip mismatch]\label{lem:lip-mismatch}
% src: ROUTE2.md §3.3 (LW1'); ROUTE2_xframe1.md §3 (LW1-x1); ROUTE2_plt1.md §3.3 (LW1'_{<1}).
For $n\ge n_0$ and $z$ on the lips in $U(0,\delta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-mismatch}
  \Bigl|\frac{D_W(z)^2}{W_0(z)}-c_\pm\Bigr|\le C\bigl(|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|)+\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\sup_{\mathrm{lips}}|d_n|\le C\varepsilon'_n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-vdiff}
  \|v-\tilde v\|_{L^\infty(\Sighat)}\le C\bigl(\varepsilon'_n+n^{-1}\bigr)\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
The convergence in \eqref{eq:he-vdiff} has no rate beyond that of $\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)$, about which nothing is
assumed.
\end{lemma}

The proof treats the three factors of $W_0$ separately. The first two are standard; the third, the slowly varying
factor, is handled by a scale-invariant path integral in the logarithmic variable, which is the point of the lemma.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 0: reduction to three ratios.} For a positive function $q$ on $(0,4c)$ with $\log q$ integrable against
$\diff x/\sqrt{x(4c-x)}$ let $D_q$ be given by \eqref{eq:he-szego} with $W_0$ replaced by $q$. Then $D_{q_1q_2}=
D_{q_1}D_{q_2}$, and $D_q^2=q$ for a positive constant $q$. Write $W_0=C_nH_n(0^+)\cdot x^\alpha\cdot q_2\cdot r_n$ with
$q_2(x):=(4c-x)H_n(x)/H_n(0^+)$. Then on a lip
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-three}
  \frac{D_W^2}{W_0}=\frac{D_{x^\alpha}(z)^2}{z^\alpha}\cdot\frac{D_{q_2}(z)^2}{q_2(z)}\cdot\frac{D_{r_n}(z)^2}{r_n(z)},
\end{equation}
where $z^\alpha$, $q_2(z)$, $r_n(z)$ are the continuations from the band. For $q$ analytic near a point $z$ off
$[0,4c]$, the identity $\frac{\mathcal R(z)}{\pi}\int_0^{4c}\frac{\diff x}{\sqrt{x(4c-x)}(z-x)}=1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-repr}
  \log\frac{D_q(z)^2}{q(z)}=\frac{\mathcal R(z)}{\pi}\int_0^{4c}\bigl(\log q(x)-\log q(z)\bigr)\frac{\diff x}{\sqrt{x(4c-x)}\,(z-x)} .
\end{equation}
On the lips in $U(0,\delta)$, by \eqref{eq:he-lipangle},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-sep}
  |\mathcal R(z)|\le C|z|^{1/2},\qquad |z-x|\ge\sin(\tfrac38\varthetalip)\,(x+|z|)\quad(x\ge0),
\end{equation}
the second because $|z-x|^2\ge(1-\cos\arg z)(x^2+|z|^2)\ge\frac12(1-\cos\arg z)(x+|z|)^2$.

\emph{Step 1: the factor $x^\alpha$.} Let $\Upsilon(z):=(z^{1/2}+(z-4c)^{1/2})^2$. It is analytic and non-zero off
$[0,4c]$ (on $(-\infty,0)$ both roots change sign and $\Upsilon$ does not), $\Upsilon(z)\sim4z$ at infinity, and
$\Upsilon_\pm(x)=(x^{1/2}\pm i(4c-x)^{1/2})^2$ on the band, so $\Upsilon_+\Upsilon_-=16c^2$. The function
$4cz/\Upsilon$ is analytic and non-zero on the simply connected set $\hat\C\setminus[0,4c]$, positive on
$\R\setminus[0,4c]$, and its two boundary values on the band are complex conjugate with product $x^2$. Taking the
logarithm that is real on $\R\setminus[0,4c]$, the function $F:=(4cz/\Upsilon)^{\alpha/2}$ satisfies $F_+F_-=x^\alpha$,
is finite and non-zero at infinity, and $|\log F|=O(\log(1/|z|))$ near $0$, $O(1)$ near $4c$. The same holds for
$D_{x^\alpha}$. The difference $g:=\log D_{x^\alpha}-\log F$ satisfies $g_++g_-=0$ on the band, so $g/\mathcal R$ has no
jump there; its singularities at $0$ and $4c$ are $O(|z-z_0|^{-1/2}\log)$, hence removable, and $g/\mathcal R\to0$ at
infinity. So $g\equiv0$ and
\[
  D_{x^\alpha}(z)^2=\Bigl(\frac{4cz}{\Upsilon(z)}\Bigr)^\alpha .
\]
Near $0$, $\Upsilon(z)=-4c\,(1+O(|z|^{1/2}))$ in both half-planes, so $4cz/\Upsilon=(-z)(1+O(|z|^{1/2}))$, and both
logarithms are real on $(-\delta,0)$. Hence $D_{x^\alpha}^2=(-z)^\alpha(1+O(|z|^{1/2}))$, and since
$(-z)^\alpha=e^{\mp i\pi\alpha}z^\alpha$ in $\C_\pm$,
\[
  \frac{D_{x^\alpha}(z)^2}{z^\alpha}=c_\pm\bigl(1+O(|z|^{1/2})\bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 2: the factor $q_2$.} By \eqref{eq:he-holder}, $|\log q_2(x)-\log q_2(z)|\le C(x^{\nu_p}+|z|^{\nu_p})$ for
$x\le2c$, and $\le C(1+|\log(4c-x)|)$ for $2c\le x<4c$. Insert this in \eqref{eq:he-repr} and use \eqref{eq:he-sep}.
The part $x\ge2c$ contributes $C|z|^{1/2}\int_{2c}^{4c}(1+|\log(4c-x)|)(4c-x)^{-1/2}\diff x\le C|z|^{1/2}$. For the part
$x\le2c$ substitute $x=|z|u$:
\[
  C|z|^{1/2}\int_0^{2c}\frac{(x^{\nu_p}+|z|^{\nu_p})\diff x}{x^{1/2}(x+|z|)}
  =C|z|^{\nu_p}\int_0^{2c/|z|}\frac{(u^{\nu_p}+1)\diff u}{u^{1/2}(1+u)}\le C|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|),
\]
since the integral converges at infinity if $\nu_p<\frac12$ and grows like $\log(1/|z|)$ if $\nu_p=\frac12$. So
$|\log(D_{q_2}^2/q_2)|\le C|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|)$.

\emph{Step 3: the slowly varying factor.} Here $\log r_n(z)=\Lambda(\xi(z))-\Lambda(\log n)$ with
$\xi(z)=\log n-\frac1p\log z$. Fix $z$ on an upper lip and $x\in(0,4c]$, and join $z$ to $x$ by the arc of radius $|z|$
from $\arg z$ to $0$, followed by the segment between $|z|$ and $x$. Every point $z'$ of this path has
$|z'|\le\max(\delta,4c)$ and $0\le\arg z'\le\frac54\varthetalip$, so
\[
  \Re\xi(z')\ge T_n,\qquad |\Im\xi(z')|\le\frac{5\varthetalip}{4p}\le\vartheta_p,
\]
the last inequality being $\frac{5\pi}{12p}<\frac\pi p$ for $p>1$, $\frac{5\pi}{12}<\frac\pi2$ for $p=1$ and
$\frac{5\pi}{16}<\frac\pi2$ for $p<1$. So the image of the path lies in $S_{\vartheta_p}\cap\{\Re\xi\ge T_n\}$, where
$|\Lambda'|\le\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)$. Its length in the $\xi$-variable is $(\arg z+|\log(x/|z|)|)/p$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-path}
  |\log r_n(x)-\log r_n(z)|\le\frac{\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)}{p}\bigl(\pi+|\log(x/|z|)|\bigr).
\end{equation}
This bound is invariant under $x\mapsto tx$, $z\mapsto tz$. Inserting it in \eqref{eq:he-repr} with \eqref{eq:he-sep},
the part $x\le2c$ is at most
\[
  C\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)|z|^{1/2}\int_0^{2c}\frac{(\pi+|\log(x/|z|)|)\diff x}{x^{1/2}(x+|z|)}
  \le C\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)\int_0^\infty\frac{(\pi+|\log u|)\diff u}{u^{1/2}(1+u)}=C'\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n),
\]
and the part $x\ge2c$ is at most $C\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)|z|^{1/2}\log(4c/|z|)\le C\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)$. The lower lips
are the mirror image. So $|\log(D_{r_n}^2/r_n)|\le C\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)$.

\emph{Step 4: conclusion.} By \eqref{eq:he-three} and Steps 1--3, $D_W^2/W_0=c_\pm e^{w}$ with
$|w|\le C(|z|^{1/2}+|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|)+\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n))\le C_0$; since $|e^w-1|\le e^{C_0}|w|$, $|c_\pm|=1$ and
$|z|^{1/2}\le|z|^{\nu_p}$, \eqref{eq:he-mismatch} follows. By \eqref{eq:he-localisation},
\[
  |d_n(z)|\le C\bigl(|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|)+\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)\bigr)e^{-n|z|^{1/2}/C}\le C\varepsilon'_n,
\]
because with $t=n|z|^{1/2}$ the first term is $t^{2\nu_p}n^{-2\nu_p}(\log2+2\log n-2\log t)e^{-t/C}\le Cn^{-2\nu_p}\log n$.
On the lips $E$ is at most $Ce^{-n/C}$ by \Fi{4}, since $\exp(-n|z|^{-1/p}/C)|z|^{-C}\le C'e^{-n/C'}$ for $|z|\le\delta$.
Together with the bounds on the circles and on the rest of $\Sigma$ stated at the end of
Section~\ref{sec:he-comparison} this gives \eqref{eq:he-vdiff}.
\end{proof}

For $\ell_1\equiv1$ we have $\varepsilon_\Lambda\equiv0$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:lip-mismatch} reduces to its H\"older part.

\subsection{Uniform invertibility}\label{sec:he-resolvent}
% src: ROUTE2.md §4.1 (UR' incl. (b')), §4.3 (C'); ROUTE2_plt1.md §4.1-4.2; RH_zeta_close.md §§1.4-1.5;
% REVIEW_Plan48 F1 and re-check.

The comparison problem is explicit, but its solution is unbounded near $0$ at the Bessel scale: on $|\zeta|\le2$ it has
size $n^{1/2}|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2}$. The following proposition says that, nevertheless, the singular integral operator
attached to it is boundedly invertible on $L^2$, uniformly in $n$.

\begin{proposition}[uniform resolvent bound for the comparison problem]\label{prop:UR-comparison}
% src: ROUTE2.md §4.1 Lemma UR' (incl. (b')); ROUTE2_plt1.md §4.1 UR'_{<1}; RH_zeta_close.md §1.4 Lemma UR.
There are $n_0$ and $C$ such that for $n\ge n_0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\tilde Q_\pm\in I+\dC(L^2(\Sighat))$ and $\tilde Q_\pm^{-1}\in I+\dC(L^2(\Sighat))$;
\item[(ii)] $\|\Cauchy_\pm\|_{L^2(\Sighat)\to L^2(\Sighat)}\le C$, and $1-\Cauchy_{\tilde v}$ is invertible on
  $L^2(\Sighat)$ with $\|(1-\Cauchy_{\tilde v})^{-1}\|\le C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:resolvent}. We describe its structure, and where $|\alpha|<1$ enters.

\emph{Reduction.} By the easy half of \ref{tool:T3}(e), which we re-prove, it suffices to solve the inhomogeneous
problem $m_+=m_-\tilde v+\mathfrak g$, $m_\pm\in\dC(L^2)$, with $\|m_\pm\|\le C\|\mathfrak g\|$, for $\mathfrak g$ in a
dense subspace of $L^2(\Sighat)$, and to show that the homogeneous problem has only the trivial solution. Uniqueness follows from
\ref{tool:T3}(b) once (i) is known. For existence we use the explicit candidate
$m:=\Cauchy(\mathfrak g\tilde Q_+^{-1})\tilde Q$, which satisfies $m_+-m_-\tilde v=\mathfrak g$ by the jump relation
$\Cauchy_+-\Cauchy_-=1$.

\emph{The uniform bound.} Away from the two discs $\tilde Q^{\pm1}$ is bounded and the bound $\|m_\pm\|\le C\|\mathfrak
g\|$ reduces to the boundedness of $\Cauchy_\pm$. Near $0$ we write $\tilde Q=A_0(2\pi)^{\sigma_3/2}n^{\sigma_3/2}
\Phi_\alpha(n^2f)$ and pass to the variable $\zeta$. The analytic prefactor contributes commutator terms with bounded
kernels, which are Hilbert--Schmidt with norms $O(1)$; this uses $\int_{|\zeta|\le3}|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}|\diff\zeta|<\infty$
on each ray, i.e.\ $|\alpha|<1$. What remains is a model operator on a star of three rays. For $|\zeta|\ge2$ it is the
Cauchy operator conjugated by $|\zeta|^{\pm1/4}$; for $|\zeta|\le3$ it is the Cauchy operator conjugated by
$|\zeta|^{\pm\alpha/2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:A-power} with $\beta=\pm\alpha/2$, which requires $|\beta|<\frac12$, i.e.\
$|\alpha|<1$), or, at $\alpha=0$, the Cauchy operator plus a logarithmic commutator (Lemma~\ref{lem:A-log}). All these
operators are dilation invariant, so their norms do not depend on $n$. The soft edge is treated in the same way, with
the Airy model in place of $\Phi_\alpha$; it involves no $\alpha$.

\emph{Membership in $\dC(L^2)$.} The candidate $m$ is a Cauchy integral times $\tilde Q$, and one has to check that it is
itself the Cauchy integral of its jump. We check this only for data $\mathfrak g$ that are $C^1$ on the arcs of
$\Sighat$ and vanish near its vertices. Then $m$ is continuous up to the contour away from the vertices, of size
$O(|z|^{-|\alpha|/2})$ at $0$, and Cauchy's integral formula, applied on the complement of the contour and of small
discs around the vertices, gives $m=\Cauchy(m_+-m_-)$. The uniform bound then extends the inverse from this dense class
to all of $L^2$. The same argument applied to $\tilde Q^{\pm1}-I$ gives (i); this is where the square integrability of
$|z|^{-|\alpha|/2}$ on the contour, i.e.\ $|\alpha|<1$, is used.

\begin{theorem}[uniform resolvent bound for the true problem]\label{thm:UR-true}
% src: ROUTE2.md §4.3 Theorem C'; RH_zeta_close.md §1.5 Theorem C; C-D Theorem 4.7 (Neumann step).
Extend $v$ by $I$ to $\Sigcirc$. There is $n_1$ such that for $n\ge n_1$ the operator $1-\Cauchy_v$ is invertible on
$L^2(\Sighat)$ and $\|(1-\Cauchy_v)^{-1}\|\le2C$, with $C$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:UR-comparison}. Moreover
$Q_\pm\in I+\dC(L^2(\Sighat))$ and $Q_-=(1-\Cauchy_v)^{-1}I$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $\Cauchy_vf-\Cauchy_{\tilde v}f=\Cauchy_-(f(v-\tilde v))$, Proposition~\ref{prop:UR-comparison}(ii) and
\eqref{eq:he-vdiff} give $\|\Cauchy_v-\Cauchy_{\tilde v}\|\le C(\varepsilon'_n+n^{-1})$, which is at most $1/(2C)$ for
large $n$. The Neumann-series step \ref{tool:T3}(d) of Conway and Deift then gives the bound. Here only the convergence
$\varepsilon'_n\to0$ is used, not its rate.

By \Fi{7}, $Q-I=\Cauchy_\Sigma(Q_+-Q_-)$. On $\Sigcirc$ the function $Q$ has no jump, and it is bounded there by \Fi{7}
(the circles stay away from $0$), so its values on $\Sigcirc$ are the boundary values of the same Cauchy integral, now
regarded on $\Sighat$ with density $0$ on $\Sigcirc$. Hence $Q_\pm\in I+\dC(L^2(\Sighat))$. Then
$Q_--I=\Cauchy_-(Q_+-Q_-)=\Cauchy_-(Q_-(v-I))$, i.e.\ $(1-\Cauchy_v)Q_-=I$ in $L^2(\Sighat)$ ($\Sighat$ has finite length,
so $I\in L^2(\Sighat)$), and $Q_-=(1-\Cauchy_v)^{-1}I$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The effect of the mismatch on the moments}\label{sec:he-moments}
% src: ROUTE2.md §4.3 Proposition D'; RH_zeta_close.md §1.6 Proposition D; ROUTE2_plt1.md §4.2 D'_{<1};
% C-D Theorem 4.4 and Proposition 4.9 via eq:CD-moments.

Write $Q=I+Q_1/z+Q_2/z^2+O(z^{-3})$ and $\Pinfz=I+P_1/z+P_2/z^2+O(z^{-3})$ at infinity; outside the discs $\tilde Q=\Pinfz$,
so $\tilde Q_k=P_k$. The circle terms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-Xi}
  \Xi_k:=-\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\Sigcirc}s^{k-1}\bigl(\tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}(s)\tilde Q_{\mathrm{out}}(s)^{-1}-I\bigr)\diff s
  \qquad(k=1,2)
\end{equation}
are $O(1/n)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison-parametrix}(iii) and \Fi{5}; they are the first-order corrections of the
comparison problem itself.

\begin{proposition}[the mismatch moves the moments by $O(\varepsilon'_n/n)$]\label{prop:moments}
% src: ROUTE2.md §4.3 Proposition D'; RH_zeta_close.md §1.6.
For $n\ge n_1$,
\begin{align*}
  Q_1&=P_1+\Xi_1+O\bigl(\varepsilon'_n/n+n^{-2}\bigr),\\
  Q_2&=P_2+\Xi_2+(Q_1-P_1)P_1+O\bigl(\varepsilon'_n/n+n^{-2}\bigr).
\end{align*}
The lip mismatch $d_n$ contributes $O(\varepsilon'_n/n)$; the remaining errors come from the interaction of the circle
terms with themselves and are $O(n^{-2})$. In particular $Q_k=P_k+O(1/n)$ for $k=1,2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We apply \ref{tool:T3}(b) and its consequence \eqref{eq:CD-moments} with $A=Q$, $B=\tilde Q$, $v_A=v$, $v_B=\tilde v$ on
$\Sighat$, $p=q=2$. The hypotheses hold: $v,\tilde v^{\pm1}\in L^\infty$, $Q_\pm\in I+\dC(L^2)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:UR-true}, and $\tilde Q_\pm^{\pm1}\in I+\dC(L^2)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:UR-comparison}(i). With
$\mathfrak h:=Q_-(v-\tilde v)\tilde v^{-1}\tilde Q_-^{-1}$, \eqref{eq:CD-moments} reads
\[
  Q_1-P_1=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Sighat}\mathfrak h\diff s,\qquad
  Q_2-P_2=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Sighat}s\,\mathfrak h\diff s+(Q_1-P_1)P_1 .
\]
Split $(v-\tilde v)\tilde v^{-1}=\Delta_1+\Delta_2+\Delta_3$, where $\Delta_1:=d_ne_2e_1^{\mathsf T}$ on the lips in
$U(0,\delta)$ (we used $e_1^{\mathsf T}\tilde v^{-1}=e_1^{\mathsf T}$ there), $\Delta_2:=\tilde v^{-1}-I$ on $\Sigcirc$, and
$\Delta_3$ is the rest: $E\tilde v^{-1}$ on the lips and $v-I$ on $\Sigma\setminus(U(0,\delta)\cup U(4c,\varepsilon)
\cup[0,4c])$. Then $\|\Delta_3\|_{L^\infty}\le Ce^{-n/C}$, on a set of length $O(n^p)$. Write $Q_-=\tilde Q_-+(Q_--\tilde
Q_-)$.

\emph{Pointwise bounds on the lips.} On the lips in $U(0,\delta)$ we have, by \eqref{eq:he-P0hat} and \eqref{eq:he-ephi},
\[
  \tilde Q_-\Delta_1\tilde Q_-^{-1}=\Bigl(\frac{D_W^2}{W_0}-c_\pm\Bigr)e^{-4\zeta^{1/2}}\,A_0(2\pi)^{\sigma_3/2}
  \Bigl[n^{\sigma_3/2}\Phi_{\alpha,-}e_2e_1^{\mathsf T}\Phi_{\alpha,-}^{-1}n^{-\sigma_3/2}\Bigr](2\pi)^{-\sigma_3/2}A_0^{-1}.
\]
If $|\zeta|\ge2$, then $n^{\sigma_3/2}\Phi_\alpha=f^{-\sigma_3/4}\Theta_\infty$ by \eqref{eq:he-Phi-large}, so the bracket
is at most $C|f|^{-1/2}\le C|z|^{-1/2}$, and $|e^{-4\zeta^{1/2}}|\le e^{-n|z|^{1/2}/C}$ by \eqref{eq:he-localisation}. If
$|\zeta|\le2$, the identity
\[
  \Phi_\alpha e_2e_1^{\mathsf T}\Phi_\alpha^{-1}=-e^{4\zeta^{1/2}}\sigma_3\Psi_\alpha e_2e_1^{\mathsf T}\Psi_\alpha^{-1}\sigma_3
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel-local}(iii) bound the bracket times
$e^{-4\zeta^{1/2}}$ by $Cn|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}$. With $\mathfrak m_n(z):=|z|^{\nu_p}\log(2/|z|)+\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)$ from
\eqref{eq:he-mismatch},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-main-bound}
  |\tilde Q_-\Delta_1\tilde Q_-^{-1}|\le C\mathfrak m_n(z)\cdot\begin{cases}n|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|},&|\zeta|\le2,\\
  |z|^{-1/2}e^{-n|z|^{1/2}/C},&|\zeta|\ge2.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the same way, $\Phi_\alpha e_2=\zeta^{-\sigma_3/4}\Theta_\infty e_2$ and $e_1^{\mathsf T}\Phi_\alpha^{-1}=e_1^{\mathsf T}
\Theta_\infty^{-1}\zeta^{\sigma_3/4}$ for $|\zeta|\ge2$, and $|\Phi_\alpha^{\pm1}|\le C|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2}$ for
$|\zeta|\le2$, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-column-bound}
  |d_n\tilde Q_-e_2|+|d_ne_1^{\mathsf T}\tilde Q_-^{-1}|\le C\mathfrak m_n(z)\cdot\begin{cases}n^{1/2}|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2},&|\zeta|\le2,\\
  |z|^{-1/4}e^{-n|z|^{1/2}/C},&|\zeta|\ge2.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=0$, replace $|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|}$ by $(1+|\log|\zeta||)^2$ and $|\zeta|^{-|\alpha|/2}$ by
$1+|\log|\zeta||$.

\emph{Bessel localisation.} We integrate \eqref{eq:he-main-bound} along the lips, on which $|\diff z|\le C\diff|z|$ and
$|\zeta|\ge n^2|z|/C$. The region $|\zeta|\le2$ lies in $|z|\le Cn^{-2}$, where $\mathfrak m_n\le C\varepsilon'_n$; its
contribution is at most
\[
  C\varepsilon'_n\int_0^{Cn^{-2}}n\,(n^2r/C)^{-|\alpha|}\diff r=\frac{C\varepsilon'_n}{n}\int_0^{C}u^{-|\alpha|}\diff u
  =O(\varepsilon'_n/n),
\]
because $|\alpha|<1$. In the region $|\zeta|\ge2$ substitute $t=nr^{1/2}$:
\begin{align*}
  \int_0^\delta\mathfrak m_n(r)r^{-1/2}e^{-nr^{1/2}/C}\diff r&=\frac2n\int_0^{n\delta^{1/2}}\mathfrak m_n(t^2/n^2)e^{-t/C}\diff t\\
  &\le\frac Cn\bigl(\varepsilon_\Lambda(T_n)+n^{-2\nu_p}\log n\bigr)=O(\varepsilon'_n/n).
\end{align*}
So $\int|\tilde Q_-\Delta_1\tilde Q_-^{-1}||\diff s|=O(\varepsilon'_n/n)$; the same computation applied to the square of
\eqref{eq:he-column-bound} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-L2-columns}
  \|d_n\tilde Q_-e_2\|_{L^2}^2+\|d_ne_1^{\mathsf T}\tilde Q_-^{-1}\|_{L^2}^2=O(\varepsilon_n'^2/n).
\end{equation}
(For $\alpha=0$ the factor $\int_0^Cu^{-|\alpha|}\diff u$ becomes $\int_0^C(1+|\log u|)^2\diff u<\infty$.)

\emph{The other main terms.} On $\Sigcirc$, $\tilde Q_-\Delta_2\tilde Q_-^{-1}=\tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}\tilde
Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}-I$, whose integrals are exactly the circle terms $\Xi_k$. The term $\Delta_3$ contributes
$O(e^{-n/C})$: on $\Sigma\setminus(U(0,\delta)\cup U(4c,\varepsilon))$, $\tilde Q^{\pm1}=(\Pinfz)^{\pm1}$ is bounded and
$|s|\le Cn^p$, and on the lips $|E||\tilde Q_-||\tilde Q_-^{-1}|\le C\exp(-n|z|^{-1/p}/C)|z|^{-C}n^{C}$, whose integral is
$O(e^{-n/C})$.

\emph{The correction term.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:UR-true} and \ref{tool:T3}(c) applied to both problems, $Q_-=I+\Cauchy_v
Q_-$ and $\tilde Q_-=I+\Cauchy_{\tilde v}\tilde Q_-$, so $(1-\Cauchy_v)(Q_--\tilde Q_-)=\Cauchy_-(\tilde Q_-(v-\tilde v))$ and
\[
  \|Q_--\tilde Q_-\|_{L^2}\le2C\,\|\Cauchy_-\|\,\|\tilde Q_-(v-\tilde v)\|_{L^2}
  \le C\bigl(\|d_n\tilde Q_-e_2\|_{L^2}+n^{-1}+e^{-n/C}\bigr)\le C\bigl(\varepsilon'_nn^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\bigr),
\]
using \eqref{eq:he-L2-columns}, the bound $O(1/n)$ for $v-\tilde v$ on the circles (where $\tilde Q_-$ is bounded), and
$\Delta_3$. Similarly $\|(v-\tilde v)\tilde v^{-1}\tilde Q_-^{-1}\|_{L^2}\le C(\varepsilon'_nn^{-1/2}+n^{-1})$. By the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
  \Bigl|\int(Q_--\tilde Q_-)(v-\tilde v)\tilde v^{-1}\tilde Q_-^{-1}\diff s\Bigr|\le C\bigl(\varepsilon'_nn^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\bigr)^2
  \le C\bigl(\varepsilon_n'^2/n+n^{-2}\bigr).
\]
The factor $s$ in the second moment is bounded on the lips and the circles, and costs at most $n^p$ on the rest, where
everything is exponentially small. Collecting terms gives the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what is not proved]\label{rem:not-proved}
% src: ROUTE2.md Theorem RII "What is proved ..." (REVIEW_Plan48 F3); OUTLINE §7 R2.
Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} isolates the non-Fisher--Hartwig part of the hard edge: relative to the comparison
problem, the lip mismatch moves $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ by $O(\varepsilon'_n/n)$. The comparison problem itself depends on
$\ell_1$ at order $1/n$. At the hard edge it does not: $\tilde Q_{\mathrm{in}}\tilde Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}=\Phat_0(\Pinfz)^{-1}$
on $\partial U(0,\delta)$ involves only $\alpha$, $f$ and $c$. But at the soft edge the Airy parametrix contains the
weight through $D_W$, so the circle terms $\Xi_k$ may depend on $\ell_1$. We do not analyse them, and we make no claim
that $b_n(\sigma_{p,s,\ell_1})/b_n(\sigma_{p,s})-1=O(\varepsilon_\Lambda/n)$. The $O(1/n)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:engine} is
absolute, not relative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the case $\alpha\ge1$]\label{rem:alpha-ge-1}
% src: ROUTE2.md §6 GAP-alpha; novelty_lineB_B §2(b) (Deift-Piorkowski quote).
For $\alpha\ge1$ the comparison frame fails at three places. The solution $\tilde Q$ is of size
$n^{1/2}|\zeta|^{-\alpha/2}$ at the Bessel scale and is not square integrable on the lips near $0$, so \ref{tool:T3}(b)
does not apply with $p=q=2$; Lemma~\ref{lem:A-power} needs $|\beta|=\alpha/2<\frac12$; and the integral
$\int_0^2u^{-\alpha}\diff u$ in the Bessel localisation diverges. (Since $s>1$, always $\alpha>-1$.) This is why the base
window $|\alpha|<1$ is combined with the Christoffel shift (Theorem~\ref{thm:shift}). It is consistent with the remark
of Deift and Piorkowski \cite[\S1]{DP} that, for a weight vanishing to first order at an endpoint, ``the crucial
Theorem 4.7 in [CD] requires a different proof in this case: now one needs to control the behaviour of the Cauchy
operator acting on spaces with Muckenhoupt weights''. An analysis of the weight itself for $\alpha\ge1$ remains open
(Section~\ref{sec:further}).
\end{remark}
