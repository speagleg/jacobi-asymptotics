\section{Numerical illustrations}\label{sec:numerics}
% src: papers/paper2/checks/table2.py -> table2.out (re-computed for this paper, 2026-10-14, in the convention of
% Section 2); cross-check against research/G1/RH/route2/checks/check_r2p_coeff.out, check_x1_coeff.out,
% check_r2_coeff.out (same b-columns; their a-column uses the pairing a_n/(2 b_{n+1})).

The computations in this section are floating-point computations; they illustrate the theorems and play no role in
their proofs. They were carried out with scripts that will be made available as supplementary material (repository
URL and commit hash: to be supplied before submission).

\emph{What is shown.} Table~\ref{tab:numerics} compares the slowly varying factor
$\ell(k)=\exp\sqrt{\log(k+e)}$, which satisfies \Ath{\vartheta_p} for every $p>0$, with the pure power $\ell\equiv1$,
in the three geometries $p=\frac12$, $p=1$ and $p=2$, all with $s=2$. By Theorems~\ref{thm:main}(iii)
and~\ref{thm:pure}, the four quantities
\[
  n\Bigl(\frac{n^pb_n}{c(p)}-1\Bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad n\Bigl(\frac{a_n}{2b_n}-1\Bigr),
\]
for $\ell$ and for $\ell\equiv1$, stay bounded. The Bessel orders are $\alpha=1$, $0$ and $-\frac12$. The first case
lies outside the window $|\alpha|<1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:engine} and is reached only through the shift
(Theorem~\ref{thm:shift}, from $s=\frac32$, where $\alpha=0$). The second is the case $\alpha=0$, which needs the
logarithmic form of the Bessel model. The third lies inside the window.

\emph{How the numbers were computed.} The recurrence coefficients cannot be computed from the atoms $k\le K$ alone.
The spectral weight of $P_n$ near the hard edge sits at indices $k\asymp n^{1+2/p}$, which is about $10^{16}$ for
$p=\frac12$ and $n=1600$, far beyond any truncation. Without a tail the truncated measure has a soft edge at its
smallest atom, and its coefficients obey a different law; for example, for $p=\frac12$, $s=\frac32$, the same $\ell$ and
the atoms $k\le2\cdot10^5$ alone, $n(n^pb_n/c(p)-1)$ equals $-1.81$, $-13.3$ and $-105.9$ at $n=100$, $400$ and
$1600$. This is a property of the truncation, not a counterexample. We therefore used the atoms $k\le K$ exactly,
together with a Gauss--Legendre quadrature of the continuous tail
$\int_{K+1/2}^{T}t^{-s}\ell(t)\,\delta_{t^{-p}}\diff t$ on logarithmic panels (ratio $1.02$, eight nodes per panel),
with $T=10^{48}$ for $p=\frac12$ and $T=10^{16}$ for $p=1,2$. The Jacobi matrix of the resulting discrete measure was
computed by an updating (RKPW-type) algorithm in extended (long double) precision.
% TODO(citation gate): Gragg--Harrod (RKPW) not read; do not cite until read (OUTLINE §6.5).
Each entry was computed for $K=10^5$ and $K=2\cdot10^5$, and for $K=2\cdot10^5$ with $T$ reduced by a factor $10^4$.
The largest change of any tabulated entry under these variations is $10^{-7}$ for $p=\frac12$, $2\cdot10^{-8}$ for
$p=1$ and $2\cdot10^{-11}$ for $p=2$, so all digits shown are stable. As a further check, the column $\ell\equiv1$ at
$p=2$ agrees in every digit shown with the closed form of Remark~\ref{rem:pure-lommel}.

\emph{What the table shows.} All entries stay bounded, as the theorems require. For the pure powers the two
normalised deviations approach $-(s-1)/2=-\frac12$ and $-p/2$ in all three geometries. For $\ell$ the deviation of
$n^pb_n$ is smaller in absolute value, and the difference from the pure power drifts slowly towards $0$, roughly like
$\frac12\Lambda'(\log n)$ with $\Lambda(\xi)=\sqrt{\log(e^\xi+e)}$; see \S\ref{sec:tilt}. The factor $\ell$ has almost
no effect on $a_n/(2b_n)$ at order $1/n$.

\section{Further remarks and open problems}\label{sec:further}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$1/n$}{1/n} term}\label{sec:tilt}
% src: ROUTE2.md §5 (conjecture + numerics); ROUTE2_xframe1.md §5; ROUTE2_plt1.md §6; RH_px §0 (pure-power
% coefficients, numerical; pairing translated to the convention of Section 2: a_n/(2b_n) has 1/n coefficient -p/2).
Theorems~\ref{thm:main}(iii) and~\ref{thm:pure} give the rate $O(1/n)$ but not the coefficient of $1/n$. The computations
of Section~\ref{sec:numerics}, and similar ones for other $s$ (for $p=\frac12$, $s\in\{1.5,1.75,2,2.5\}$; for $p=1$,
$s\in\{2,2.5,3\}$), suggest the following.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:tilt}
\textup{(a)} For every $p>0$ and $s>1$, the pure powers satisfy
\[
  \frac{n^pb_n(\sigma_{p,s})}{c(p)}=1-\frac{s-1}{2n}+o\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr),\qquad
  \frac{a_n(\sigma_{p,s})}{2b_n(\sigma_{p,s})}=1-\frac{p}{2n}+o\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
\textup{(b)} If $\ell$ satisfies \Ath{\vartheta_p} with $\ell(k)=\exp\Lambda(\log k)$, then
\[
  \frac{n^pb_n(\sigma_{p,s,\ell})}{c(p)}=1-\frac{s-1-\Lambda'(\log n)}{2n}+o\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda'(\log n)}{n}\Bigr)
  \qquad\text{whenever}\quad n\Lambda'(\log n)\to\infty.
\]
\end{conjecture}

Part (a) holds for $(p,s)=(2,2)$, by the closed form of Remark~\ref{rem:pure-lommel} (granted the identification made
there). Part (b) says that, at order $1/n$, a slowly varying factor acts like a change of the exponent $s$ by the local
logarithmic derivative $\Lambda'(\log n)$, i.e.\ like a hard-edge Bessel order that drifts with $n$. In
Table~\ref{tab:numerics} the difference between the first two columns is $0.91$ to $0.99$ times
$\frac12\Lambda'(\log n)$, and this ratio increases with $n$ in all three geometries. The $1/n$
coefficient behaves well under the shift: by the identities of Section~\ref{sec:shift}, (a) at $s$ implies (a) at $s+p$
provided the chain-sequence parameters of Section~\ref{sec:shift} have increments
$n^pu_n-(n-1)^pu_{n-1}=o(1/n)$, a condition we can only check numerically.
% src: Remark SH-1/n (ROUTE2_alpha); implication proved, hypothesis numerical (OUTLINE §5, Remark 3.7).
Within our method, (b) amounts to computing the main term of the comparison estimate of Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}
(as Conway and Deift do for their weight \cite[(5.5)]{CD}), which would need a Bessel parametrix with a drifting order.

\subsection{Other routes at \texorpdfstring{$p=1$}{p = 1}}\label{sec:p1-routes}
% src: ROUTE2_p1.md, ROUTE2_xframe1.md §0; PROOF.md §3.22 "Relation to §3.23" (REVIEW_Plan53 F1-F2);
% RH_px §4.1, RH_plt1b §2; ROUTE2_xframe1 Lemma X2.
At $p=1$ the nodes $1/k$ become $\pm k^{-1/2}$ in the variable $y=\pm x^{1/2}$, which is the variable of
\cite{WLXZ}. We also carried out the analysis in that symmetric frame, where $0$ is an interior point of the band with
a Fisher--Hartwig exponent $s-\frac32$. The comparison method then needs this exponent in $(-\frac12,\frac12)$, so it
covers only $1<s<2$; the loss is an effect of the frame, since $L^2(|\diff y|)$ on a symmetric contour corresponds to
$L^2(|x|^{-1/2}|\diff x|)$ in $x$. Folding the symmetric problem gives a second proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:engine} at
$p=1$ for $1<s<3$. It shares the decisive comparison step at $\alpha=0$ with Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}, so it is not
independent where it matters, and we do not include it. Finally, for pure powers and $p\ne1$ the classical route also
works: one can glue a local Bessel parametrix built from a continuation of the band weight and conclude by a small-norm
argument. At $p=1$ that construction fails (Remark~\ref{rem:quotient-p1}); the comparison argument is not affected,
because it uses only boundary values of $D_W$ and never continues the weight across $(-\delta,0)$.

\subsection{Rough weights}\label{sec:rough}
The announcement in \S\ref{sec:context} shows that the pointwise law can fail for weights $k^{-s}e^{f(k)}$ with
$|f|\le\varepsilon$, although the averaged law holds for them. Two questions remain. First, between slow variation and
arbitrary bounded oscillation there is a range of regularity conditions on $f$ for which we do not know the answer. A
simple test case is $f(k)=\varepsilon\sin\log k$: its increments $f(k+1)-f(k)$ tend to $0$, but $e^{f}$ is not
slowly varying, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does not apply. Second, for bounded rough $f$ one may ask whether the law
holds along a subsequence of density one; we do not know.

\subsection{Other nodes, larger Bessel orders, and zeta zeros}\label{sec:nodes}
% src: PROOF.md §4 "Open now"; ROUTE2 §6 GAP-alpha; outline_A_nodes.md (barrier; not cited).
Our nodes are exact powers $k^{-p}$. For nodes $x_k=f(k)$ with $f$ regularly varying of index $-p$ the averaged law
is known \cite{Paper1}, but the pointwise law is open. Heuristically one expects $b_n/f(n)\to c(p)$ under smooth
regular variation of $f$, and failure under rough displacements of the nodes, in analogy with the weights.

For Bessel orders $\alpha\ge1$ we pass through the shift (Theorem~\ref{thm:shift}). An RH analysis of the weight itself
for $\alpha\ge1$ would require a different comparison frame (Remark~\ref{rem:alpha-ge-1}); it would be needed to compute
the $1/n$ term directly in that range.

A natural measure outside our scope is built from the ordinates $\gamma_k$ of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta
function, taken as given real numbers with multiplicity, for instance with atoms $1/(\gamma_k^2+\frac14)$. Its atoms
are not of the form $k^{-p}$ times a slowly varying factor with regular spacing: the fluctuations of the gaps
$\gamma_{k+1}-\gamma_k$ act like a rough perturbation of the nodes, which is the kind of perturbation that the
discussion above suggests can destroy pointwise laws. Moreover, the pointwise law for this measure would imply the
averaged one, and we expect, from work in preparation, that the averaged law there carries information on the
multiplicities of the ordinates that we do not know how to prove. We therefore regard this case as a barrier for the
present methods rather than as a target.
