\title[Recurrence coefficients with power-law atoms]{Recurrence coefficients of discrete measures with power-law
atoms: pointwise asymptotics and the $1/n$ term}
\author{Gordon [Surname]}
\date{Draft of 15 October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Let $\sigma=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-s}\ell(k)\,\delta_{k^{-p}}$ with $p>0$ and $s>1$, so that its Jacobi matrix is a compact
operator with eigenvalues $k^{-p}$. A previous paper established that the recurrence coefficients satisfy
$n^pb_n\to c(p)$ in a double Ces\`aro sense, with an explicit constant $c(p)$ that comes from a constrained equilibrium
problem. Here we prove the pointwise law $n^pb_n=c(p)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$ for every $p>0$ and $s>1$,
both for pure powers ($\ell\equiv1$) and for every factor $\ell$ that extends to a slowly varying analytic function in a
sector $|\arg t|\le\vartheta$, however small $\vartheta>0$ is. For pure powers we also find the $1/n$ term for every
$p>0$: $n^pb_n=c(p)(1-(s-1)/(2n)+\dotsb)$ and $n^pa_n=2c(p)(1-(s-1+p)/(2n)+\dotsb)$, with remainder $O(n^{-2})$ for
$p\ge1$; for $0<p<1$ both brackets carry the further term $\mathsf k_pn^{-1-p}$, $\mathsf k_p:=2p\,c(p)\zeta(-p)$,
which cancels in $a_n/(2b_n)$. The proof
is a Riemann--Hilbert analysis of discrete orthogonal polynomials whose nodes accumulate at a hard edge. The outer scalar
function depends on whether $p$ is larger than, equal to or smaller than $1$. At the hard edge a non-analytic slowly
varying factor is handled by a comparison, in the manner of Conway and Deift, with an explicitly solvable Bessel model;
this needs the Bessel order $(s-1-p)/p$ to lie in $(-1,1)$. A Christoffel transformation, which shifts $s$ by $p$ and
preserves the law together with its rate, removes the restriction. For pure powers with $p\ne1$ the classical Bessel
parametrix applies; at $p=1$ we pass to the symmetric variable $x^{1/2}$, in which the hard edge becomes an interior
Fisher--Hartwig point, and fix the value of the $1/n$ term by an elementary comparison with the Tricomi--Carlitz
polynomials, which also gives the second-order constant $-\frac1{24}$ at $s=\frac32$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A compact Jacobi operator is determined by its spectral measure. If its eigenvalues are $k^{-p}$ and its spectral
weights decay like $k^{-s}$, how do its matrix entries behave? For $p>0$, $s>1$ and a sequence
$\ell\colon\N\to(0,\infty)$ we study the discrete measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
  \sigma_{p,s,\ell}:=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-s}\ell(k)\,\delta_{k^{-p}},\qquad \sigma_{p,s}:=\sigma_{p,s,1}.
\end{equation}
It lives on $(0,1]$, and its atoms accumulate only at $0$. Let $P_n$ be its monic orthogonal polynomials and
$h_n:=\int P_n^2\diff\sigma$. They satisfy the three-term recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi}
  xP_n=P_{n+1}+a_nP_n+b_n^2P_{n-1}\qquad(n\ge0,\ b_0:=0,\ b_n>0),
\end{equation}
so that $b_n^2=h_n/h_{n-1}$; the numbers $a_n$ and $b_n$ are the entries of the Jacobi matrix of $\sigma$. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp}
  I(p):=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac1p+\frac12)}{\Gamma(\frac1p+1)},\qquad
  c(p):=\frac14\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2I(p)}\Bigr)^{p},
\end{equation}
for example $c(1)=\frac12$ and $c(2)=\pi^2/16$, the question is whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
  n^pb_n\longrightarrow c(p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad n^pa_n\longrightarrow2c(p)\qquad(n\to\infty),
\end{equation}
and at what rate. We call \eqref{eq:law} the \emph{pointwise law}. The constant $c(p)$ comes from the first paper of this series
\cite{Paper1}, which proves the first half of \eqref{eq:law} in an averaged sense (see \eqref{eq:paper1-averaged}
below).

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}
The slowly varying factors we allow are those that extend analytically to a sector, written in the logarithmic
variable $\xi=\log t$.

\begin{definition}[Analytic slowly varying factors]\label{def:analytic}
Let $\vartheta>0$. A sequence $\ell\colon\N\to(0,\infty)$ is \emph{analytic of angle $\vartheta$} if
$\ell(k)=\exp\Lambda(\log k)$ for all $k\ge1$, where, for some $c_0>0$, the function $\Lambda$ is analytic on
$S_\vartheta:=\{\xi\in\C:\ \re\xi\ge-c_0,\ |\im\xi|\le\vartheta\}$, real on $S_\vartheta\cap\R$, bounded on
$S_\vartheta\cap\{\re\xi\le0\}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsLambda}
  \varepsilon_\Lambda(T):=\sup\bigl\{|\Lambda'(\xi)|:\ \xi\in S_\vartheta,\ \re\xi\ge T\bigr\}\longrightarrow0
  \qquad(T\to\infty).
\end{equation}
We then also say that $\Lambda$ is analytic of angle $\vartheta$.
\end{definition}

Such an $\ell$ is slowly varying. The angle may be arbitrarily small, because the Riemann--Hilbert analysis evaluates
the weight only in a sector whose opening we are free to shrink (Lemma~\ref{lem:weightdomain} and
Remark~\ref{rem:angles}). For example, $\exp\sqrt{\log(k+e)}$ and $(\log(k+e))^\beta$, $\beta\in\R$, are analytic of
every angle $\vartheta<\pi$, while $\Lambda(\xi)=\sqrt{\xi+1}+(\xi+2)^{-1/2}\log(1-q\cos2\xi)$ with $q=1/\cosh(0.6)$
has logarithmic branch points at $\xi=\pi m\pm0.3i$, $m\in\mathbb Z$, and is analytic only of the angles
$\vartheta<0.3$ (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic}
Let $p>0$ and $s>1$, and let $\ell$ be analytic of angle $\vartheta$ for some $\vartheta>0$. Then the recurrence
coefficients of $\sigma_{p,s,\ell}$ satisfy, as $n\to\infty$,
\[
  n^pb_n=c(p)\bigl(1+O(1/n)\bigr),\qquad \frac{a_n}{2b_n}=1+O(1/n),
\]
with constants depending on $p$, $s$ and $\ell$.
\end{theorem}

The constant sequence $\ell\equiv1$ is analytic of every angle, with $\Lambda\equiv0$.

\begin{corollary}[Pure powers]\label{cor:pure}
For every $p>0$ and $s>1$ the measure $\sigma_{p,s}$ satisfies $n^pb_n=c(p)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$.
In particular, for real $q>0$ and $r$ with $2q+r>1$, the measure $\sigma_{q,2q+r}$ has the moments
$\zeta(qj+2q+r)$, $j\ge0$, and Hankel determinants $\det_{1\le i,j\le n}\zeta(q(i+j)+r)$
\textup{(}\cite[Corollary~1.4 and its proof]{Paper1}\textup{)}, and its recurrence coefficients satisfy
$n^qb_n=c(q)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$.
\end{corollary}

For pure powers we identify the next term. For $0<p<1$ put $\mathsf k_p:=2p\,c(p)\,\zeta(-p)$, where $\zeta$ is the
Riemann zeta function; $\mathsf k_p<0$ for every $p\in(0,1)$, and for instance
$\mathsf k_{1/2}=\zeta(-\frac12)/\sqrt6=-0.08487\ldots$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:onen}
Let $p>0$ and $s>1$, and let $a_n$, $b_n$ be the recurrence coefficients of $\sigma_{p,s}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] If $p\ge1$, then, as $n\to\infty$,
\[
  n^pb_n=c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1}{2n}+O(n^{-2})\Bigr),\qquad
  n^pa_n=2c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1+p}{2n}+O(n^{-2})\Bigr).
\]
Consequently $a_n/(2b_n)=1-p/(2n)+O(n^{-2})$ and $b_n=c(p)\bigl(n+\frac{s-1}{2p}\bigr)^{-p}\bigl(1+O(n^{-2})\bigr)$.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] If $0<p<1$, then, as $n\to\infty$,
\begin{align*}
  n^pb_n&=c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1}{2n}+\frac{\mathsf k_p}{n^{1+p}}+O\bigl(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2}\bigr)\Bigr),\\
  n^pa_n&=2c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1+p}{2n}+\frac{\mathsf k_p}{n^{1+p}}+O\bigl(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2}\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{align*}
Consequently $a_n/(2b_n)=1-p/(2n)+O(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2})$, and, since $\mathsf k_p\ne0$, the remainder after the $1/n$ term
of $n^pb_n$ and of $n^pa_n$ is of exact order $n^{-1-p}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Here, as everywhere in this paper, $a_n/(2b_n)$ pairs the diagonal entry at $P_n$ with the off-diagonal entry $b_n$
between $P_{n-1}$ and $P_n$, as in \eqref{eq:jacobi}.

The proofs use the following external results, each stated with its hypotheses in
\S\S\ref{sec:tool-wlxz}--\ref{sec:tool-dlmf} (formulas used only for Remark~\ref{rem:lommel} are quoted where they are
used). For Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic}: the formulation of the discrete Riemann--Hilbert problem of Wu, Lin, Xu and Zhao
\cite{WLXZ}, as a pattern only; the Bessel model of Kuijlaars, McLaughlin, Van Assche and Vanlessen \cite[\S6]{KMVV};
Theorem~4.4 of Conway and Deift \cite{CD}, together with the argument of their Theorem~4.7 and Proposition~4.9 and one
direction of their Proposition~4.6, which we re-prove; and special-function facts from \cite{DLMF}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} uses in addition the classical Bessel parametrix \cite[(6.42)--(6.53)]{KMVV}, and at $p=1$ the
steepest-descent analysis of the Tricomi--Carlitz problem \cite[\S\S3--6]{WLXZ}, with the weight multiplied by an
explicit analytic factor, and the Fisher--Hartwig parametrix of Kuijlaars and Vanlessen \cite[\S5]{KV} in the form of
Vanlessen \cite[\S4]{Van}. In particular, at $p=1$ it relies on two steps of the analysis of \cite{WLXZ} that we do not
prove again: the exponential bounds for the jumps on the lens and saturated contours, uniformly up to the largest node,
which are justified there only briefly, and the uniform boundedness of the Cauchy operator on the final contour used in
their small-norm argument (Section~\ref{sec:pone}). The Laurent coefficients used for $p\ne1$ were computed by hand and checked by
exact symbolic expansion, and the Tricomi--Carlitz coefficients used at $p=1$ by exact computer algebra.

\begin{remark}[Scope at order $1/n$]\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} does not identify the $1/n$ term. Relative to the exactly solvable comparison problem of
Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}, the non-analytic part of $\ell$ moves the first two coefficients at infinity by $o(1/n)$,
but the comparison problem itself depends on $\ell$ at order $1/n$. So the $O(1/n)$ is absolute: we do not claim that
$b_n(\sigma_{p,s,\ell})/b_n(\sigma_{p,s})-1=O(\varepsilon_\Lambda(\log n)/n)$. Conjecture~\ref{conj:tilt} proposes the
$1/n$ term for $\ell\not\equiv1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The case $(p,s)=(2,2)$]\label{rem:lommel}
For $(p,s)=(2,2)$ the coefficients are explicit:
\[
  b_n=\frac{\pi^2}{(4n+1)\sqrt{(4n-1)(4n+3)}},\qquad a_n=\frac{2\pi^2}{(4n+1)(4n+5)}\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
They come from the orthonormal Lommel polynomials of order $\frac32$ \cite[\S3]{VA} by a quadratic transformation; the
derivation is given at the end of Remark~\ref{rem:second}. Thus $n^2b_n/c(2)=1-\frac1{2n}+O(n^{-2})$ and
$a_n/(2b_n)=1-\frac1n+O(n^{-2})$, an independent test of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} at $(p,s)=(2,2)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Second order at $p=1$]\label{rem:tc-intro}
At $p=1$ and $s\in\{\frac32,\frac52\}$ the measure $\sigma_{1,s}$ can be compared with the Tricomi--Carlitz measure and
its Christoffel transform, whose recurrence coefficients are explicit. This gives the second-order terms:
$b_n/b_n^{\mathrm{ref}}=1-\frac1{24n^2}+O(n^{-3})$, the same for $a_n$, and
$nb_n=\frac12\bigl(1-\frac1{4n}+\frac5{96n^2}+O(n^{-3})\bigr)$ for $s=\frac32$,
$nb_n=\frac12\bigl(1-\frac3{4n}+\frac{53}{96n^2}+O(n^{-3})\bigr)$ for $s=\frac52$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:tc}). In
particular the remainder $O(n^{-2})$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen}(i) cannot be improved in general. The comparison is
elementary and is also the starting point of our proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} at $p=1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Context}\label{sec:context}
\emph{From the averaged law to the pointwise law.} By \cite[Corollary~1.2]{Paper1}, specialised to the atoms
$x_k=k^{-p}$, if the weights $w_k\ge0$ satisfy $\sum_kw_k<\infty$ and $w_k\ge c_1k^{-s'}$ for some $c_1>0$ and
$s'\in\R$, then the recurrence coefficients of $\sum_kw_k\delta_{k^{-p}}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper1-averaged}
  \frac{2}{n^2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\log\bigl(j^pb_j\bigr)\longrightarrow\log c(p).
\end{equation}
The constant comes from an explicitly solvable constrained equilibrium problem \cite[Theorem~1.3]{Paper1}, whose
solution $\mu^*$ is the zero distribution of a Jacobi matrix with entries $a_n\approx2b_n\approx2c(p)n^{-p}$. But
\eqref{eq:paper1-averaged} is a statement about the Hankel determinant $D_n$ of $\sigma$ at order $n^2$, and multiplying
the weights by factors in $[e^{-\varepsilon},e^{\varepsilon}]$ changes $\log D_n$ by at most $\varepsilon n$. Passing to
\eqref{eq:law} is therefore a Tauberian step, and the regularity of $j^pb_j$ that it needs has to come from the weights:
bounded rough weights can break the pointwise law although the averaged law holds for them \cite{Paper3}. That paper
also uses the theorems proved here to treat general slowly varying weights.

\emph{Two difficulties.} First, the atoms accumulate at $0$, which is a hard edge of $\mu^*$ (its density behaves like
$x^{-1/2}$ there). After the rescaling $z=n^px$ used below, the band $(0,4c(p))$ in which the polynomials oscillate
contains infinitely many nodes near its left end, a situation not covered by the Riemann--Hilbert theory of discrete
orthogonal polynomials on finite node sets \cite{BKMM}. To our knowledge, the only Riemann--Hilbert analysis of discrete
orthogonal polynomials on infinitely many nodes accumulating at a finite point is that of \cite{WLXZ}, for the
Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials, an observation also made by Dai and Yao \cite[\S1]{DY}; our frame follows theirs. Second,
a slowly varying factor enters the rescaled weight through $r_n(z):=\ell(nz^{-1/p})/\ell(n)$, which is not analytic at
the hard edge $z=0$, so the standard local parametrix does not exist there.

\emph{Non-analytic weights.} Conway and Deift \cite{CD} met the second difficulty for the weight $\log\frac{2k}{1-x}$ on
$(-1,1)$; they write that ``there is no known parametrix for the Riemann--Hilbert problem in a neighborhood of the
logarithmic singularity at $x=1$'', and they compare the problem with the Legendre problem through the operator theory
behind Riemann--Hilbert problems. The comparison argument of Section~\ref{sec:hardedge} is theirs \cite[Theorem~4.7 and
Proposition~4.9]{CD}. We add a comparison model built from the Szeg\H{o} function of the full weight, a uniform resolvent
bound for a Bessel edge of any order $|\alpha|<1$, and the treatment of an arbitrary analytic slowly varying factor.
Deift and Piorkowski \cite{DP} extended \cite{CD} to the weight $\log\frac{2}{1-x}$ and write, about a comparison with a
weight that has a simple zero at the endpoint: ``the crucial Theorem~4.7 in [4] requires a different proof in this
case: now one needs to control the behaviour of the Cauchy operator acting on spaces with Muckenhoupt weights''
\cite[\S1]{DP}, [4] being \cite{CD}. This is consistent with our restriction to $|\alpha|<1$ in the comparison step
(Remark~\ref{rem:alpha-ge-1}). Other approaches are Piorkowski's parametrix-free method, which needs an a priori
estimate for the exact solution \cite{Pio}; the $\bar\partial$ methods of McLaughlin and Miller \cite{MM} and of
Trogdon \cite{Tro}, which assume finitely many Lipschitz derivatives of the weight; the approximation of a smooth symbol
by analytic ones in Deift, Its and Krasovsky \cite[\S6.2]{DIK}; and, for exponential weights $e^{-Q}$, the $o(1)$
asymptotics of Levin and Lubinsky \cite{LL}, without Riemann--Hilbert methods.

\emph{Special cases.} The Hankel determinants of zeta values in Corollary~\ref{cor:pure} were studied by
Monien \cite{Monien} and by Haynes and Zudilin \cite{HaynesZudilin}; for $q=1$ Monien reports a full asymptotic
expansion, found numerically by D.~Zagier and not proved. The Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials, orthogonal on the points $\pm1/\sqrt{k+t_0}$,
have explicit recurrence coefficients \cite[\S3]{VA} and uniform asymptotics \cite{WLXZ}; for $t_0=1$ the masses differ from those of $\sigma_{1,3/2}$ by a factor
$1+1/(12k)+O(k^{-2})$, which is the basis of Corollary~\ref{cor:tc} and of our treatment of $p=1$.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:strategy}
The elementary part of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} is the Christoffel shift, Theorem~\ref{thm:shift}: the
pointwise law passes from $\sigma$ to $x\sigma$ together with any regular rate, and $x\sigma_{p,s,\ell}=\sigma_{p,s+p,\ell}$.
The statement without rate follows from \cite[Proposition~2.1]{DPQ} and the transfer formulas \cite[\S1]{DD}; the rate is
new. The shift reduces the theorem to $1<s<1+2p$, where the Bessel order $\alpha:=(s-1-p)/p$ of the hard edge lies in
$(-1,1)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}). In the variable $z=n^px$, where $\mu^*$ has the band $(0,4c(p))$ and the saturated
region $(4c(p),\infty)$, we build one Riemann--Hilbert frame for all $p>0$ (Section~\ref{sec:frame}). The cases $p>1$, $p=1$ and $p<1$ differ only in the outer function that removes the
Gamma-function growth of the node masses (Section~\ref{sec:outer}). At the hard
edge we compare the problem, following Conway and Deift, with a Bessel model built from the Szeg\H{o} function of the
whole band weight; the two differ on the lips of the lens by $o(1)$, and for $|\alpha|<1$ a uniform resolvent bound and
Bessel localisation turn this into an $O(1/n)$ error in the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}).

For the $1/n$ term of pure powers the classical Bessel parametrix is available. For $p>1$ the global parametrix
contributes exactly $c(p)^2$ and $2c(p)$ to the functionals that extract $b_n^2$ and $a_n$, the hard-edge correction is
annihilated by both, and the $1/n$ term comes from the Airy correction at the soft edge (Sections~\ref{sec:classical}
and~\ref{sec:second}). For $0<p<1$ the band factor is $\exp(-2\zeta(-p)x+O(x^2))$ up to constants; it enters
through the Airy correction, at order $n^{-1-p}$ and equally in $b_n$ and $a_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:plt1}). At $p=1$ the
classical construction fails at the hard edge (Remark~\ref{rem:quotient-p1}). We then work in the symmetric variable
$x^{1/2}$ of \cite{WLXZ}, where $0$ is an interior Fisher--Hartwig point, and show that the recurrence coefficients of the
symmetrised measure have a $1/N$ term whose alternating part cancels when they are folded back. Its value is fixed by the
comparison with the Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials at $s=\frac32$ and an elementary bound on $\log h_n(s)-\log h_n(t)$
(Section~\ref{sec:pone}).

\subsection{Organisation}\label{sec:organisation}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes conventions and states the external results, and Section~\ref{sec:shift} proves the
Christoffel shift. Sections~\ref{sec:frame} and~\ref{sec:outer} build the Riemann--Hilbert frame for all $p>0$ and
$\alpha>-1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}), and Sections~\ref{sec:hardedge} and~\ref{sec:extraction} complete the proofs
of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} and Corollary~\ref{cor:pure}; a reader interested only in these can stop there.
Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} is proved in Sections~\ref{sec:classical} and~\ref{sec:second} for $p>1$, in
Section~\ref{sec:plt1} for $0<p<1$ and in Section~\ref{sec:pone} for $p=1$; the last is largely independent of
Sections~\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:plt1}. Sections~\ref{sec:numerics} and~\ref{sec:further} give numerical
illustrations and open problems.

Throughout, $n$ is the degree, and $C,C'$ denote positive constants that may change from line to line; the letter $c$
is reserved for $c(p)$, except for $c_0$ in Definition~\ref{def:analytic} and $c_1$ in~\eqref{eq:paper1-averaged}.
