\title[Recurrence coefficients with power-law atoms]{Recurrence coefficients of discrete measures with power-law
atoms: pointwise asymptotics and the $1/n$ term}
\author{Gordon [Surname]}
\date{Draft of 15 October 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Let $\sigma=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-s}\ell(k)\,\delta_{k^{-p}}$ with $p>0$ and $s>1$, so that its Jacobi matrix is a compact
operator with eigenvalues $k^{-p}$. A previous paper established that the recurrence coefficients satisfy
$n^pb_n\to c(p)$ in a double Ces\`aro sense, with an explicit constant $c(p)$ that comes from a constrained equilibrium
problem. Here we prove the pointwise law $n^pb_n=c(p)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$ for every $p>0$ and $s>1$,
both for pure powers ($\ell\equiv1$) and for every factor $\ell$ that extends to a slowly varying analytic function in a
sector $|\arg t|\le\vartheta$, however small $\vartheta>0$ is. For pure powers we also find the $1/n$ term for every
$p>0$: $n^pb_n=c(p)(1-(s-1)/(2n)+\dotsb)$ and $n^pa_n=2c(p)(1-(s-1+p)/(2n)+\dotsb)$, with remainder $O(n^{-2})$ for
$p\ge1$; for $0<p<1$ both carry the further term $2p\,c(p)\zeta(-p)\,n^{-1-p}$, which cancels in $a_n/(2b_n)$. The proof
is a Riemann--Hilbert analysis of discrete orthogonal polynomials whose nodes accumulate at a hard edge. The outer scalar
function depends on whether $p$ is larger than, equal to or smaller than $1$. At the hard edge a non-analytic slowly
varying factor is handled by a comparison, in the manner of Conway and Deift, with an explicitly solvable Bessel model;
this needs the Bessel order $(s-1-p)/p$ to lie in $(-1,1)$. A Christoffel transformation, which shifts $s$ by $p$ and
preserves the law together with its rate, removes the restriction. For pure powers with $p\ne1$ the classical Bessel
parametrix applies; at $p=1$ we pass to the symmetric variable $x^{1/2}$, in which the hard edge becomes an interior
Fisher--Hartwig point, and fix the value of the $1/n$ term by an elementary comparison with the Tricomi--Carlitz
polynomials, which also gives the second-order constant $-\frac1{24}$ at $s=\frac32$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A compact Jacobi operator is determined by its spectral measure. If its eigenvalues are $k^{-p}$ and its spectral
weights decay like $k^{-s}$, how do its matrix entries behave? For $p>0$, $s>1$ and a sequence
$\ell\colon\N\to(0,\infty)$ we study the discrete measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
  \sigma_{p,s,\ell}:=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-s}\ell(k)\,\delta_{k^{-p}},\qquad \sigma_{p,s}:=\sigma_{p,s,1}.
\end{equation}
It lives on $(0,1]$, and its atoms accumulate only at $0$. Let $P_n$ be its monic orthogonal polynomials and
$h_n:=\int P_n^2\diff\sigma$. They satisfy the three-term recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi}
  xP_n=P_{n+1}+a_nP_n+b_n^2P_{n-1}\qquad(n\ge0,\ b_0:=0,\ b_n>0),
\end{equation}
so that $b_n^2=h_n/h_{n-1}$; the numbers $a_n$ and $b_n$ are the entries of the Jacobi matrix of $\sigma$. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp}
  I(p):=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac1p+\frac12)}{\Gamma(\frac1p+1)},\qquad
  c(p):=\frac14\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2I(p)}\Bigr)^{p},
\end{equation}
for example $c(1)=\frac12$ and $c(2)=\pi^2/16$, the question is whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
  n^pb_n\longrightarrow c(p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad n^pa_n\longrightarrow2c(p)\qquad(n\to\infty),
\end{equation}
and at what rate. We call \eqref{eq:law} the \emph{pointwise law}. The constant $c(p)$ comes from the first paper of this series
\cite{Paper1}, which proves the first half of \eqref{eq:law} in an averaged sense (see \eqref{eq:paper1-averaged}
below).

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}
The slowly varying factors we allow are those that extend analytically to a sector, written in the logarithmic
variable $\xi=\log t$.

\begin{definition}[Analytic slowly varying factors]\label{def:analytic}
Let $\vartheta>0$. A sequence $\ell\colon\N\to(0,\infty)$ is \emph{analytic of angle $\vartheta$} if
$\ell(k)=\exp\Lambda(\log k)$ for all $k\ge1$, where, for some $c_0>0$, the function $\Lambda$ is analytic on
$S_\vartheta:=\{\xi\in\C:\ \re\xi\ge-c_0,\ |\im\xi|\le\vartheta\}$, real on $S_\vartheta\cap\R$, bounded on
$S_\vartheta\cap\{\re\xi\le0\}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsLambda}
  \varepsilon_\Lambda(T):=\sup\bigl\{|\Lambda'(\xi)|:\ \xi\in S_\vartheta,\ \re\xi\ge T\bigr\}\longrightarrow0
  \qquad(T\to\infty).
\end{equation}
We then also say that $\Lambda$ is analytic of angle $\vartheta$. If $\vartheta'<\vartheta$, a sequence that is
analytic of angle $\vartheta$ is also analytic of angle $\vartheta'$.
\end{definition}

Such an $\ell$ is slowly varying. The angle may be arbitrarily small, because the Riemann--Hilbert analysis evaluates
the weight only in a sector whose opening we are free to shrink (Lemma~\ref{lem:weightdomain} and
Remark~\ref{rem:angles}). For example, $\exp\sqrt{\log(k+e)}$ and $(\log(k+e))^\beta$, $\beta\in\R$, are analytic of
every angle $\vartheta<\pi$, while $\Lambda(\xi)=\sqrt{\xi+1}+(\xi+2)^{-1/2}\log(1-q\cos2\xi)$ with $q=1/\cosh(0.6)$
has logarithmic branch points at $\xi=\pi m\pm0.3i$, $m\in\mathbb Z$, and is analytic only of the angles
$\vartheta<0.3$ (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic}
Let $p>0$ and $s>1$, and let $\ell$ be analytic of angle $\vartheta$ for some $\vartheta>0$. Then the recurrence
coefficients of $\sigma_{p,s,\ell}$ satisfy, as $n\to\infty$,
\[
  n^pb_n=c(p)\bigl(1+O(1/n)\bigr),\qquad \frac{a_n}{2b_n}=1+O(1/n),
\]
with constants depending on $p$, $s$ and $\ell$.
\end{theorem}

The constant sequence $\ell\equiv1$ is analytic of every angle, with $\Lambda\equiv0$.

\begin{corollary}[Pure powers]\label{cor:pure}
For every $p>0$ and $s>1$ the measure $\sigma_{p,s}$ satisfies $n^pb_n=c(p)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$.
In particular, for real $q>0$ and $r$ with $2q+r>1$, the measure $\sigma_{q,2q+r}$ has the moments
$\zeta(qj+2q+r)$, $j\ge0$, and Hankel determinants $\det_{1\le i,j\le n}\zeta(q(i+j)+r)$
\textup{(}\cite[Corollary~1.4 and its proof]{Paper1}\textup{)}, and its recurrence coefficients satisfy
$n^qb_n=c(q)(1+O(1/n))$ and $a_n/(2b_n)=1+O(1/n)$.
\end{corollary}

For pure powers we identify the next term. For $0<p<1$ put $\mathsf k_p:=2p\,c(p)\,\zeta(-p)$, where $\zeta$ is the
Riemann zeta function; $\mathsf k_p<0$ for every $p\in(0,1)$, and for instance
$\mathsf k_{1/2}=\zeta(-\frac12)/\sqrt6=-0.08487\ldots$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:onen}
Let $p>0$ and $s>1$, and let $a_n$, $b_n$ be the recurrence coefficients of $\sigma_{p,s}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] If $p\ge1$, then, as $n\to\infty$,
\[
  n^pb_n=c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1}{2n}+O(n^{-2})\Bigr),\qquad
  n^pa_n=2c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1+p}{2n}+O(n^{-2})\Bigr).
\]
Consequently $a_n/(2b_n)=1-p/(2n)+O(n^{-2})$ and $b_n=c(p)\bigl(n+\frac{s-1}{2p}\bigr)^{-p}\bigl(1+O(n^{-2})\bigr)$.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] If $0<p<1$, then, as $n\to\infty$,
\begin{align*}
  n^pb_n&=c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1}{2n}+\frac{\mathsf k_p}{n^{1+p}}+O\bigl(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2}\bigr)\Bigr),\\
  n^pa_n&=2c(p)\Bigl(1-\frac{s-1+p}{2n}+\frac{\mathsf k_p}{n^{1+p}}+O\bigl(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2}\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{align*}
Consequently $a_n/(2b_n)=1-p/(2n)+O(n^{-1-2p}+n^{-2})$, and, since $\mathsf k_p\ne0$, the remainder after the $1/n$ term
of $n^pb_n$ and of $n^pa_n$ is of exact order $n^{-1-p}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Here, as everywhere in this paper, $a_n/(2b_n)$ pairs the diagonal entry at $P_n$ with the off-diagonal entry $b_n$
between $P_{n-1}$ and $P_n$, as in \eqref{eq:jacobi}.

The proofs use the following external results, each stated with its hypotheses in
\S\S\ref{sec:tool-wlxz}--\ref{sec:tool-dlmf} (the few formulas used only in the derivation of
Remark~\ref{rem:lommel} are quoted where they are used). For Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic}: the formulation of the discrete
Riemann--Hilbert problem by Wu, Lin, Xu and Zhao \cite{WLXZ}, as a pattern only (none of their theorems is applied);
the explicit Bessel model of Kuijlaars, McLaughlin, Van Assche and Vanlessen \cite[\S6]{KMVV}, including its freedom
in the choice of angle; Theorem~4.4 of Conway and Deift \cite{CD}, together with the argument of their Theorem~4.7
and Proposition~4.9 and one direction of their Proposition~4.6, which we re-prove; and special-function facts from
\cite{DLMF}. Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} uses in addition the classical left-endpoint Bessel parametrix of
\cite[(6.42)--(6.53)]{KMVV} and the expansions \cite[\S\S9.7, 10.17, 10.40]{DLMF}; for $0<p<1$ also the integrals and
the reflection formula \cite[(5.2.1), (25.2.1), (25.4.1)]{DLMF}. At $p=1$ the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} does apply
the analysis of \cite{WLXZ}: it runs their steepest-descent analysis of the Tricomi--Carlitz problem
\cite[\S\S3--6]{WLXZ}, with the weight multiplied by an explicit analytic factor, and uses the orthogonality of the
Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials as stated in \cite[(1.4)--(1.9)]{WLXZ} and the Fisher--Hartwig parametrix of Kuijlaars and
Vanlessen \cite[\S5]{KV} in the form of Vanlessen \cite[\S4]{Van}. In particular, at $p=1$ it relies on the bounds of
\cite{WLXZ} for the jumps on the saturated region, which are uniform up to the largest node and are justified there only
briefly (Section~\ref{sec:pone}). The Laurent coefficients of the first corrections that enter the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} for $p\ne1$ (Sections~\ref{sec:second}, \ref{sec:plt1} and Appendices~\ref{app:residues},
\ref{app:plt1-residues}) were computed by exact symbolic series expansion, and those that enter the proof were also
computed by hand; the explicit coefficients of the Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials used at $p=1$ were checked by exact
computer algebra.

\begin{remark}[Scope at order $1/n$]\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} bounds the deviation from $c(p)$ at order $1/n$ but does not identify the $1/n$ term. The
proof compares the true problem with an exactly solvable comparison problem: relative to it, the non-analytic part of
$\ell$ moves the first two coefficients at infinity by $o(1/n)$ (Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}), but the comparison
problem itself depends on $\ell$ at order $1/n$. So the $O(1/n)$ is absolute, not relative: we do not claim that
$b_n(\sigma_{p,s,\ell})/b_n(\sigma_{p,s})-1=O(\varepsilon_\Lambda(\log n)/n)$. A conjecture for the $1/n$ term when
$\ell\not\equiv1$ is formulated in Section~\ref{sec:further} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:tilt}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The case $(p,s)=(2,2)$]\label{rem:lommel}
For $(p,s)=(2,2)$ the coefficients are explicit:
\[
  b_n=\frac{\pi^2}{(4n+1)\sqrt{(4n-1)(4n+3)}},\qquad a_n=\frac{2\pi^2}{(4n+1)(4n+5)}\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
They come from the orthonormal Lommel polynomials of order $\frac32$ \cite[\S3]{VA} by a quadratic transformation; the
derivation is given at the end of Remark~\ref{rem:second}. Thus $n^2b_n/c(2)=1-\frac1{2n}+O(n^{-2})$ and
$a_n/(2b_n)=1-\frac1n+O(n^{-2})$, in agreement with Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} at $(p,s)=(2,2)$. The closed form does not
use the Riemann--Hilbert analysis, so it is an independent test of the $1/n$ term.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Second order at $p=1$]\label{rem:tc-intro}
At $p=1$ and $s\in\{\frac32,\frac52\}$ the measure $\sigma_{1,s}$ can be compared with the Tricomi--Carlitz measure and
its Christoffel transform, whose recurrence coefficients are explicit. This gives the second-order terms:
$b_n/b_n^{\mathrm{ref}}=1-\frac1{24n^2}+O(n^{-3})$, the same for $a_n$, and
$nb_n=\frac12\bigl(1-\frac1{4n}+\frac5{96n^2}+O(n^{-3})\bigr)$ for $s=\frac32$,
$nb_n=\frac12\bigl(1-\frac3{4n}+\frac{53}{96n^2}+O(n^{-3})\bigr)$ for $s=\frac52$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:tc}). In
particular the remainder $O(n^{-2})$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen}(i) cannot be improved in general. The comparison is
elementary and is also the starting point of our proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} at $p=1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Context}\label{sec:context}
\emph{From the averaged law to the pointwise law.} By \cite[Corollary~1.2]{Paper1}, specialised to the atoms $x_k=k^{-p}$, if
the weights $w_k\ge0$ satisfy $\sum_kw_k<\infty$ and $w_k\ge c_1k^{-s'}$ for some constants $c_1>0$ and $s'\in\R$,
then the recurrence coefficients of $\sum_kw_k\delta_{k^{-p}}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper1-averaged}
  \frac{2}{n^2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\log\bigl(j^pb_j\bigr)\longrightarrow\log c(p).
\end{equation}
The constant comes from an explicitly solvable constrained equilibrium problem \cite[Theorem~1.3]{Paper1}, whose
solution $\mu^*$ is the zero distribution that a Jacobi matrix with entries $a_n\approx2b_n\approx2c(p)n^{-p}$ would
produce. But \eqref{eq:paper1-averaged} is a double Ces\`aro mean of $\log(j^pb_j)$, that is, a statement about the
logarithm of the Hankel determinant $D_n$ of $\sigma$ at order $n^2$. Multiplying the weights by factors in
$[e^{-\varepsilon},e^{\varepsilon}]$ changes $\log D_n$ by at most $\varepsilon n$, so the averaged law cannot see such
perturbations. Passing from it to \eqref{eq:law} is a Tauberian step, and the regularity of $j^pb_j$ that this step
needs has to come from the weights. That this is not a technicality is shown in the third paper of the series \cite{Paper3}:
bounded rough weights can break the pointwise law although the averaged law holds for them. That paper also uses the
theorems proved here, as black boxes, to treat general slowly varying weights.

\emph{Two difficulties.} A Riemann--Hilbert proof of \eqref{eq:law} meets two obstacles. First, the atoms accumulate at
$0$, which is a hard edge of $\mu^*$ (its density behaves like $x^{-1/2}$ there). After the rescaling $z=n^px$ used
below, the band $(0,4c(p))$ in which the polynomials oscillate contains infinitely many nodes near its left end, a
situation not covered by the Riemann--Hilbert theory of discrete orthogonal polynomials on finite node sets. Second, a
slowly varying factor enters the rescaled weight through $r_n(z):=\ell(nz^{-1/p})/\ell(n)$, which is not analytic at the
hard edge $z=0$, so the standard local parametrix does not exist there.

\emph{Discrete orthogonal polynomials.} The Riemann--Hilbert approach to discrete orthogonal polynomials was developed
by Baik, Kriecherbauer, McLaughlin and Miller \cite{BKMM} for measures on finite node sets in a bounded interval, with
real-analytic node density and potential; their analysis includes saturated regions, which also appear here. To our knowledge, the only Riemann--Hilbert
analysis of discrete orthogonal polynomials on infinitely many nodes accumulating at a finite point is that of Wu, Lin, Xu and Zhao \cite{WLXZ}, for the Tricomi--Carlitz
polynomials, an observation also made by Dai and Yao \cite[\S1]{DY}; our frame follows theirs.

\emph{Non-analytic weights.} Conway and Deift \cite{CD} met the second difficulty for the weight $\log\frac{2k}{1-x}$ on $(-1,1)$, which
carries a slowly varying factor at the endpoint $x=1$; they write that ``there is no known parametrix for the
Riemann--Hilbert problem in a neighborhood of the logarithmic singularity at $x=1$'', and they compare the problem with
the Legendre problem through the operator theory behind Riemann--Hilbert problems instead of through a quotient. The
comparison argument of Section~\ref{sec:hardedge} is theirs \cite[Theorem~4.7 and Proposition~4.9]{CD}. What we add
is a comparison model built from the Szeg\H{o} function of the full weight (an explicit parametrix rather than a second
orthogonality problem), a uniform resolvent bound for a Bessel edge of any order $|\alpha|<1$ (rather than the Legendre
value $\alpha=0$), and the treatment of an arbitrary analytic slowly varying factor (rather than one explicit
logarithm). Deift and Piorkowski \cite{DP} extended the analysis of \cite{CD} to the weight $\log\frac{2}{1-x}$ and write, about a comparison with a weight that has a simple
zero at the endpoint: ``the crucial Theorem~4.7 in [4] requires a different proof in this case: now one needs to
control the behaviour of the Cauchy operator acting on spaces with Muckenhoupt weights'' \cite[\S1]{DP}, [4] being
\cite{CD}. This is consistent with our restriction to Bessel orders $|\alpha|<1$ in the comparison step
(Remark~\ref{rem:alpha-ge-1}). Other approaches to non-analytic weights are Piorkowski's parametrix-free method, which
needs an a priori estimate for the exact solution \cite{Pio}; the $\bar\partial$ methods of McLaughlin and Miller
\cite{MM} and of Trogdon \cite{Tro}, which assume a finite number of Lipschitz derivatives of the weight (for
\cite{Tro}, $C^{k,1}$ regularity up to the endpoints of the support); and the approximation of a smooth symbol by
analytic ones in Deift, Its and Krasovsky \cite[\S6.2]{DIK}.

\emph{Recurrence asymptotics without analyticity.} For exponential weights $W=e^{-Q}$, Levin and Lubinsky \cite{LL}
prove $o(1)$ asymptotics of the recurrence coefficients, with constants given by equilibrium quantities, under
regularity conditions that do not involve analyticity, and without Riemann--Hilbert methods.

\emph{Special cases.} Besides the Lommel case (Remark~\ref{rem:lommel}), we mention the Hankel determinants of zeta
values studied by Monien \cite{Monien} and by Haynes and Zudilin \cite{HaynesZudilin}; for $q=1$ in the notation of Corollary~\ref{cor:pure}, Monien reports a full
asymptotic expansion of the determinants, obtained numerically by D.~Zagier, which goes beyond
Corollary~\ref{cor:pure} but is not proved. The Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials are orthogonal with respect to a
measure on the points $\pm1/\sqrt{k+t_0}$, for a parameter $t_0>0$ \cite[\S3]{VA}; in the squared variable this is an instance of $p=1$ with weights that are not pure
powers. Their recurrence coefficients are explicit \cite[\S3]{VA}, and \cite{WLXZ} obtain uniform asymptotics of the
polynomials. For $t_0=1$ the masses differ from those of $\sigma_{1,3/2}$ by a factor $1+1/(12k)+O(k^{-2})$; this
is the basis of Corollary~\ref{cor:tc} and of our treatment of $p=1$.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:strategy}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} has an elementary part and a Riemann--Hilbert part. The elementary part is the
Christoffel shift, Theorem~\ref{thm:shift}: the pointwise law passes from $\sigma$ to $x\sigma$ together with any
regular rate, and $x\sigma_{p,s,\ell}=\sigma_{p,s+p,\ell}$. What is new there is the rate; the statement without rate
follows from a proposition of Dai, Piao and Qiao \cite[Proposition~2.1]{DPQ} and the classical transfer formulas
\cite[\S1]{DD}. The shift reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} to the window $1<s<1+2p$, where the Bessel order
$\alpha:=(s-1-p)/p$ of the hard edge lies in $(-1,1)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}). For the Riemann--Hilbert part we
work in the rescaled variable $z=n^px$. There the equilibrium measure $\mu^*$ of \cite{Paper1} has the band $(0,4c(p))$, where the
polynomials oscillate, the saturated region $(4c(p),\infty)$, where their zeros follow the nodes, and a soft edge at
$4c(p)$, and on it we build one frame for all $p>0$ (Section~\ref{sec:frame}). The three geometries $p>1$, $p=1$ and
$p<1$ enter only through the outer scalar function that removes the Gamma-function growth of the node masses
(Section~\ref{sec:outer} and Appendix~\ref{app:outer}): the index function $t(z)=nz^{-1/p}$ maps the slit plane onto a
sector that lies inside, exactly fills, or exceeds the sector where Stirling's formula holds. At the hard edge the
classical Bessel parametrix is not available, because $r_n$ is not analytic at $0$. Following Conway and Deift, we compare the problem with a Bessel model built from the
Szeg\H{o} function of the whole band weight; the two problems differ on the lips of the lens by $o(1)$ without a rate,
and for $|\alpha|<1$ a uniform resolvent bound and Bessel localisation turn this into an $O(1/n)$ error in the
coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:hardedge}).

For the $1/n$ term of pure powers we use the classical Bessel parametrix instead. For $p>1$ it applies for every
$\alpha>-1$ (Section~\ref{sec:classical}). The global parametrix contributes exactly $c(p)^2$ and $2c(p)$ to the
functionals that extract $b_n^2$ and $a_n$, so the $1/n$ terms come from the first corrections of the local models;
the band factor is constant up to $O(n^{-p})$, the hard-edge correction is annihilated by both functionals, and the
$1/n$ term comes from the Airy correction at the soft edge (Section~\ref{sec:second}). For $0<p<1$ the square root of the
weight is glued across the two rays of Appendix~\ref{app:outer}, at the cost of super-exponentially small jumps, and the
same scheme works, but the band factor is no longer constant to $o(1/n)$: in the variable $x$ it is
$\exp(-2\zeta(-p)x+O(x^2))$ up to constants, the coefficients being moments of the jump across the two rays. This factor
enters only through the Airy correction, so at order $n^{-1}\cdot n^{-p}$, and equally in $b_n$ and $a_n$
(Section~\ref{sec:plt1}). At $p=1$ the classical construction fails at the hard edge (Remark~\ref{rem:quotient-p1}). We
then work in the symmetric variable $x^{1/2}$ of \cite{WLXZ}, where $0$ is an interior Fisher--Hartwig point of the band,
and show that the recurrence coefficients of the symmetrised measure have a $1/N$ term, with an alternating part that
cancels when they are folded back. The value of the $1/n$ term is then fixed without residue computations, by the
elementary comparison with the Tricomi--Carlitz polynomials at $s=\frac32$ and an elementary bound on
$\log h_n(s)-\log h_n(t)$ (Section~\ref{sec:pone}).

\subsection{Organisation}\label{sec:organisation}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} fixes conventions and states every external result used later, and
Section~\ref{sec:shift} proves the Christoffel shift, independently of the rest. Sections~\ref{sec:frame}
and~\ref{sec:outer} build the Riemann--Hilbert frame for all $p>0$ and $\alpha>-1$; Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}
collects everything the later sections use from it. Sections~\ref{sec:hardedge} and~\ref{sec:extraction} treat the hard
edge by comparison for $|\alpha|<1$ and complete the proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:pure}. Sections~\ref{sec:classical} and~\ref{sec:second} prove Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} for $p>1$,
Section~\ref{sec:plt1} for $0<p<1$ and Section~\ref{sec:pone} for $p=1$; Section~\ref{sec:classical} also states a
localisation bound for the spectral weights, used in \cite{Paper3} and proved in Appendix~\ref{app:tail}.
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} gives numerical illustrations and Section~\ref{sec:further} conjectures and open problems.
A reader interested only in Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} can stop after Section~\ref{sec:extraction} (with
Appendices~\ref{app:outer}--\ref{app:resolvent}). For Theorem~\ref{thm:onen} with $p>1$ one needs
Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}, \ref{sec:frame}, \ref{sec:outer}, \ref{sec:classical} and~\ref{sec:second} with
Appendices~\ref{app:outer}, \ref{app:apriori} and~\ref{app:residues}; from Section~\ref{sec:hardedge} only the
normalised Bessel model of \S\ref{sec:he-model} and the identity~\eqref{eq:he-ephi}. The case $0<p<1$ needs in addition
Section~\ref{sec:plt1} and Appendix~\ref{app:plt1-residues}. The case $p=1$ is largely independent of
Sections~\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:plt1}: it needs Section~\ref{sec:pone} and Appendix~\ref{app:pone}, the lemmas of
Section~\ref{sec:shift} on the transfer formulas and the chain sequence, and Corollary~\ref{cor:pure}.

Throughout, $n$ is the degree, and $C,C'$ denote positive constants that may change from line to line. They are
never written $c$, which is reserved for $c(p)$; the exceptions are $c_0$ in Definition~\ref{def:analytic} and $c_1$ in
\eqref{eq:paper1-averaged}. Our atoms are exactly $k^{-p}$, so the atom constant of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Paper1} does not
appear.
