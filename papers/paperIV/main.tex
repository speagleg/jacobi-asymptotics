\title[Jacobi coefficients of measures on reciprocal spectra]{Jacobi coefficients of measures on reciprocal spectra: a
spectral dictionary and the natural index}
\author{Gordon Speagle}
\date{October 2026}

\subjclass[2020]{Primary 42C05; Secondary 47B36, 31A15, 30E15, 11M35}
\keywords{Orthogonal polynomials, Jacobi matrices, recurrence coefficients, discrete measures, constrained equilibrium,

\begin{abstract}
For an increasing sequence $\Lambda=\{\lambda_m\}$ with $\lambda_m\sim m^p$ we study the recurrence coefficients
$a_n,b_n$ of the discrete measure $\sum_m\lambda_m^{-s/p}\delta_{1/\lambda_m}$; the lattice $\lambda_m=m^p$ gives the
measures $\sum_kk^{-s}\delta_{k^{-p}}$ of the earlier papers of this series, for which $n^pb_n\to c(p)$. For the lattice
and every $p\ne1$ we prove an expansion of $n^pb_n/c$ to order $n^{-7}+n^{-2-3p}$. It is a double series in $1/n$
and in $\zeta(-jp)n^{-jp}$, computed by a finite residue recursion and exact computer algebra. In the natural index $N=n+\frac\alpha2+\frac12$, $\alpha=(s-1-p)/p$, the integer
layer is even in $1/N$ to all orders: separating the roles of $\alpha$ as Bessel order and as Szeg\H{o} exponent, we prove a
reflection symmetry and, by steepest descent in a second frame of non-integer index, that the Szeg\H{o} exponent enters only
through $N$. The first-order zeta terms are $\varepsilon_jN^{-1-jp}(1+g_jN^{-2})$ with
$\varepsilon_j=\binom{2j}{j}p\,c^j\zeta(-jp)$ and $g_j$ quadratic in $\alpha$, for all $j$, and, formally, the zeta layers are Toda flows of
the integer layer. For Hurwitz lattices $\lambda_m=(m+\delta)^p$, $-1<\delta\le0$, the offset only shifts the Szeg\H{o} exponent, so the lattice
expansion holds with $N$ shifted by $\delta$ and $\zeta(-jp)$ replaced by $\zeta(-jp,1+\delta)$; for
$\lambda_m=(m+\delta)^p+\kappa$, $p>1$, we prove the first pole term. We write the zeta layer as a Mellin--Barnes integral
with kernel $\pi[\cot(\pi wp/2)-\cot(\pi w)]$ and solve the constrained equilibrium problem for a general decreasing node
density explicitly; its soft edge carries every pole of the node zeta function with the weight $\pi\cot(\pi q)$. For general
spectra these statements become a conjectural dictionary between the expansion of $b_n$ and the spectral zeta function of
$\Lambda$, which we test numerically on perturbed lattices, Bessel zeros and spectra with multiplicities (with predictions
fixed before the measures were computed), and on planar drums and anharmonic oscillators; for the oscillators $-\frac{\mathrm d^2}{\mathrm dq^2}+q^N$ the
predicted $n^{-2}$ shift is the rational number $(N-1)/(6(N+2))$.
\end{abstract}
