## 1. Main results

**Theorem E3.1 (p > 1).** Let p > 1 and s > 1. Then

  n^pb_n/c(p) = 1 − (s−1)/(2n) + d₂(p, s)/n² + κ_pn^{−1−p} + O(n⁻³),   κ_p := 2p c(p)ζ(−p).

For p ≥ 2 the κ_p term is itself O(n⁻³) (and κ₂ = 0); the content is the case 1 < p < 2, where n⁻³ < n^{−1−p} < n⁻². This
replaces the remainder O(n^{−min(3, 1+p)}) of E2 Theorem E2.1 by O(n⁻³) for every p > 1.

**Theorem E3.2 (0 < p < 1).** Let 0 < p < 1, s > 1 and K := ⌊1/p⌋ (so ν_K = 1 + (K+1)p > 2). There are constants
ε_j = ε_j(p, s) (1 ≤ j ≤ K) such that

  n^pb_n/c(p) = 1 − (s−1)/(2n) + Σ_{j=1}^K ε_jn^{−1−jp} + d₂(p, s)n^{−2} + O(n^{−2−p} + n^{−ν_K}),

where d₂ is the closed form of E2 Theorem E2.1, and

  ε₁ = κ_p = 2pcζ(−p),  ε₂ = 6pc²ζ(−2p),  ε₃ = 20pc³ζ(−3p)  (whenever 2 ≤ K, resp. 3 ≤ K).

(ε₁ is E_plt1 Theorem E′.1; the others are new.) More precisely ε_j is the weighted-degree-j part of e(μ_n) (Lemma E3.7,
Prop E3.6), a polynomial in ζ(−p), …, ζ(−jp) with coefficients depending on p, c and possibly s.

**Corollary E3.3 (d₂ for 0 < p < 1).** (i) If 1/p ∉ ℕ, the exponents 1 + jp (j ≤ K) differ from 2, and the n⁻² coefficient of
n^pb_n/c is d₂(p, s) = (p+1)(s−1)²/(8p) − (p+2)(s−1−p)²/(8p²) + (p+1)/12 — the same closed form as for p > 1.
(ii) If p = 1/j₀ (j₀ ≥ 2), the n^{−1−j₀p} and n⁻² terms merge, and the n⁻² coefficient is d₂ + ε_{j₀}. Since c(1/j)^j = 1/C(2j, j)
exactly (I(1/j) = (π/2)C(2j, j)4^{−j}), this is d₂ − 1/24 at p = 1/2 and d₂ − 1/36 at p = 1/3, i.e. d₂ − p/12 in both cases.

**Corollary E3.4 (the n^{−1−2p}, n^{−1−3p} coefficients for 0 < p < 1).** (i) For 0 < p < 1, p ≠ 1/2, the coefficient of
n^{−1−2p} in n^pb_n/c is ε₂ = 6pc(p)²ζ(−2p). (ii) For 0 < p < 1/2, p ≠ 1/3, the coefficient of n^{−1−3p} is ε₃ = 20pc(p)³ζ(−3p).
(At p = 1/2, resp. 1/3, these terms merge with n⁻²: Corollary E3.3(ii).) The n^{−1−2p} coefficient was listed as open in E_plt1
§10 and Paper II §13.1 ("the first-order response to this quadratic factor plus the second-order response to the linear one"):
the second-order response to the linear factor vanishes (Lemma E3.7), and the first-order response to the quadratic factor is
6cpμ₂ = 6pc²ζ(−2p)n^{−2p}.

**Conjecture E3.C.** e(μ) = cp·M(4c) for every polynomial tilt (no nonlinear terms at order 1/n), hence ε_k = C(2k, k)p c^kζ(−kp)
for all k, the formal sum being Σ_kC(2k, k)(c/n^p)^kζ(−kp) = "Σ_m[(1 − 4cm^p/n^p)^{−1/2} − 1]" (zeta-regularised; HEURISTIC),
and at p = 1/j the merged n⁻² coefficient is d₂ − p/12. At p = 1 the last statement is E2 Remark E2.10 (d₂ − 1/12; PROVED-mod-cite
at s ∈ {3/2, 5/2}, NUMERICAL otherwise).

## 2. Lemma F.1′: the band factor for p > 1

Notation of E_pgt1 Lemma E.2: G = −j(t) + log B, H_n = e^{−2G}, 𝔢 the jump density on (−∞, 0), R₁ = 4c + 2, and (E.2.1)
G(z) − G(1) = (1/2π)∫_{−∞}^0𝔢(y)(z − 1)/((y − z)(y − 1))dy.

**Lemma F.1′ (PROVED-mod-cite: E_pgt1 Lemma E.2 [REVIEWED]; DLMF 5.9.1 (μ = 1), 25.2.1, 25.4.1 as quoted in RH/CITATIONS.md
"(DLMF-zeta, Plan 67)" and "(Plan E3)").** Let p > 1.
(i) For k ≥ 1, m_k := (1/2π)∫_{−∞}^0𝔢(y)y^{−k−1}dy = ζ(−kp)/(kn^{kp}).
(ii) For every K ≥ 1 there is C = C(p, K) with |G(z) − G(1) − Σ_{k≤K}m_k(z^k − 1)| ≤ Cn^{−(K+1)p} for |z| ≤ R₁ (boundary values on
  (−R₁, 0) included), and the derivative bounds of E_pgt1 Lemma E.2(ii) hold for the left side with n^{−p} replaced by n^{−(K+1)p}.
Hence log H_n(z) − log H_n(1) = −2Σ_{k≤K}m_k(z^k − 1) + O(n^{−(K+1)p}), i.e. H_n ∝ e^{−P_n(z)}e^{−2E_n^{(K)}(z)} with
P_n(x) := Σ_{k≤K}μ_{n,k}x^k, μ_{n,k} = 2m_k = 2ζ(−kp)/(kn^{kp}), E_n^{(K)} = O(n^{−(K+1)p}) — the same law as E_plt1 Lemma F.1 for
p < 1. At p = 2 all m_k vanish (ζ(−2k) = 0), consistent with H_n being exactly constant (E_pgt1 Remark E.2(3)).

*Proof.* (i) On (−∞, 0), y = −r, y₊ = re^{iπ}, so t₊ = nr^{−1/p}e^{−iπ/p} and q(r) := e^{−2πit₊} = exp(−aₙr^{−1/p}) with
aₙ := 2πin e^{−iπ/p}, Re aₙ = 2πn sin(π/p) > 0 (p > 1). So |q| < 1, Log(1 − q) = −Σ_{m≥1}q^m/m and 𝔢 = 2Im Log(1 − q) =
−2Im Σ_m q^m/m. Since Σ_m(1/m)∫_0^∞|q|^m r^{−k−1}dr < ∞ (below), Fubini applies. Substituting w = r^{−1/p} (r^{−k−1}dr =
p w^{kp−1}dw up to orientation) and using DLMF 5.9.1 with μ = 1, ν = kp (Re ν > 0) and z = maₙ (Re z > 0; principal power):
∫_0^∞q^m r^{−k−1}dr = p∫_0^∞e^{−maₙw}w^{kp−1}dw = pΓ(kp)(maₙ)^{−kp}. The principal argument of aₙ is π/2 − π/p ∈ (−π/2, π/2), so
(maₙ)^{−kp} = (2πmn)^{−kp}e^{−ikp(π/2 − π/p)} = (2πmn)^{−kp}e^{−iπkp/2}(−1)^k. (With |·|: Σ_m(1/m)pΓ(kp)|maₙ|^{−kp} < ∞, which
is the Fubini bound.) Summing with DLMF 25.2.1: ∫_0^∞𝔢(−r)r^{−k−1}dr = −2Im[pΓ(kp)ζ(1+kp)(2πn)^{−kp}(−1)^ke^{−iπkp/2}] =
2(−1)^kpΓ(kp)ζ(1+kp)(2πn)^{−kp}sin(πkp/2). With y^{−k−1} = (−1)^{k+1}r^{−k−1} and dy = −dr over (−∞, 0) ↔ (0, ∞):
m_k = −(1/π)pΓ(kp)ζ(1+kp)(2πn)^{−kp}sin(πkp/2), which is ζ(−kp)/(kn^{kp}) by DLMF 25.4.1 exactly as in the last step of E_plt1
Lemma F.1(iii). (Numerically: check_e3_moments.out, 40 digits, including the zeros at p = 2 and at k = 2, p = 3.)
(ii) For |y| ≥ 2R₁ and |z| ≤ R₁, (z − 1)/((y − z)(y − 1)) = Σ_{k≤K}(z^k − 1)y^{−k−1} + O(|y|^{−K−2}) uniformly. Split (E.2.1) and the
m_k at |y| = 2R₁: the near parts are O(e^{−cn}) with derivatives (E_pgt1 Lemma E.2(c), unchanged); the far part of the remainder is
≤ C∫|𝔢||y|^{−K−2}dy ≤ C′n^{−(K+1)p} (substitute |y| = n^pw as in E_pgt1 Lemma E.2(c), with |𝔢| ≤ π e^{−2π sin(π/p)n|y|^{−1/p}}).
Derivatives: differentiate under the integral (far part) and Cauchy's estimate (as in E_pgt1 Lemma E.2(ii)). ∎

## 3. Lemma E3.4: tilted Szegő data and the gauge

For μ ∈ ℂ^K let P_μ(x) := Σ_{k≤K}μ_kx^k, q_μ := e^{−P_μ}, and

  Q_k(z) := Σ_{j=0}^{k−1}z^{k−1−j}C(2j, j)c^j,  M_μ(z) := Σ_kμ_kQ_k(z)   (Q₁ = 1, Q₂ = z + 2c, Q₃ = z² + 2cz + 6c²).

**Lemma E3.4 (PROVED).** (i) For real μ and z ∉ [0, 4c]: log D_{q_μ}(z)² − log q_μ(z) = 𝓡(z)M_μ(z). Hence for the weight
x^α(4c−x)q_μ: ω^μ = ω^{mod}e^{𝓡M_μ}, (−ω^μ)^{1/2} = (−ω^{mod})^{1/2}e^{𝓡M_μ/2}, (ρ^μ)² = (ρ^{mod})²e^{−𝓡M_μ}.
(ii) G_μ(z) := P₀^∞(z)e^{−𝓡(z)M_μ(z)σ₃/2}P₀^∞(z)^{−1} extends to an entire function of (z, μ) ∈ ℂ × ℂ^K with det G_μ = 1, G₀ = I.
(iii) For the Airy and Bessel jump data of E_pgt1 Lemma E.3 and E2 Lemma E2.3 (k = 1, 2) computed with the weight
x^α(4c−x)q_μ: Δ_k^{⋄,μ} = G_μΔ_k^{⋄,mod}G_μ^{−1} (⋄ ∈ {0, r}). In particular Δ_k^{⋄,μ} is meromorphic in the disc with the pole
orders of Δ_k^{⋄,mod} (E2 Lemma E2.6(a), E_pgt1 Lemma E.5), and all its Laurent coefficients are entire functions of μ.

*Proof.* (i) Paper 2 (eq. szego-repr) with 𝔠 = log q_μ: log D² − 𝔠(z) = (𝓡/π)∫_0^{4c}(𝔠(x) − 𝔠(z))dx/(√(x(4c−x))(z − x)), and
(𝔠(x) − 𝔠(z))/(z − x) = Σ_kμ_k(x^k − z^k)/(x − z) = Σ_kμ_kΣ_{j<k}x^jz^{k−1−j}; the arcsine moments (1/π)∫_0^{4c}x^jdx/√(x(4c−x)) =
C(2j, j)c^j (x = 4c sin²φ) give 𝓡M_μ. (μ = (λ) is E_plt1 Lemma F.2.) The ω, ρ statements follow from Paper 2 (eq. omega) and
D_{q₁q₂} = D_{q₁}D_{q₂}, as in E_plt1 §4.
(ii) With h := 𝓡M_μ/2, T^{−1}e^{−hσ₃}T = [[cosh h, −sinh h], [−sinh h, cosh h]], so
G_μ = T[[cosh h, −𝔞_∞²sinh h], [−𝔞_∞^{−2}sinh h, cosh h]]T^{−1}. cosh h and S := sinh(h)/𝓡 are even in 𝓡, hence entire functions of
𝓡² = z(z−4c) (and of μ); 𝔞_∞²𝓡 = z − 4c and 𝔞_∞^{−2}𝓡 = z (the branches agree at ∞ and both sides are analytic off [0, 4c]).
So G_μ = T[[cosh h, −(z − 4c)S], [−zS, cosh h]]T^{−1} with all four entries entire in (z, μ); det = cosh²h − (z−4c)zS² =
cosh²h − sinh²h = 1.
(iii) The Airy datum Δ_k^r = (−1/φ̃)^kP₀^∞(−ω)^{−σ₃/2}X_k(−ω)^{σ₃/2}(P₀^∞)^{−1}: by (i), (−ω^μ)^{∓σ₃/2} = e^{∓𝓡M_μσ₃/2}(−ω^{mod})^{∓σ₃/2}
(diagonal matrices commute), and inserting (P₀^∞)^{−1}P₀^∞ gives G_μΔ_k^{r,mod}G_μ^{−1}. The Bessel datum
Δ_k^0 = (2f^{1/2})^{−k}P₀^∞ρ^{σ₃}(σ₃Ξ_kσ₃)ρ^{−σ₃}(P₀^∞)^{−1}: by (i), (ρ^μ)^{σ₃} = e^{−𝓡M_μσ₃/2}(ρ^{mod})^{σ₃}, the same G_μ. ∎

(This is the structural reason behind E_plt1 Lemma E′.5's meromorphy for the linear tilt, now for all polynomial tilts and both
orders. Lemma E3.7 shows that, to weighted order 3, the gauge changes the extracted 1/n coefficient only linearly.)

## 4. Lemma E3.5: the circle jumps with the band factor

**Lemma E3.5 (PROVED-mod-cite: E_pgt1 Lemma E.3, E_plt1 Lemma E′.3, E2 Lemma E2.3 [all REVIEWED]; Lemma F.1 (p < 1), F.1′
(p > 1)).** Let p ≠ 1, K ≥ 1. Uniformly on both circles (boundary values included),

  P̂(P₀^∞)^{−1} = I + Δ₁/n + Δ₂/n² + O(n⁻³),  Δ_k = G_{μ_n}Δ_k^{mod}G_{μ_n}^{−1} + O(n^{−(K+1)p})  (k = 1, 2).

*Proof.* The expansions are E2 Lemma E2.3 (p > 1) and its p < 1 version: the local Bessel/Airy computations are p-free, and for
p < 1 the Bessel sectors are those of E_plt1 Lemma E′.3 (rays π − θ_L; |ph w| ≤ π/2 − θ_L/2 for DLMF 10.40.1–4, and
ph 2(−ζ)^{1/2} ∈ (−θ_L/2, θ_L/2) for 10.17.5–6, .11–.12), with the three-term truncations of E2 Lemma E2.3 (the sector
argument of E2 Lemma E2.3 (review A B2) is valid for every k and these sectors); RH_plt1b's glue changes the prefactor by O(e^{−c′n}).
The n-dependence: by Lemma F.1(iv) (p < 1) resp. F.1′(ii) (p > 1), the band weight is 𝗐′_n z^α(4c−z)q_{μ_n}(z)e^{−2E_n^{(K)}(z)} with
|E_n^{(K)}| ≤ Cn^{−(K+1)p} and the derivative bounds stated there; as in E_plt1 Lemma E′.3 ("The n-dependence", with E_n replaced
by E_n^{(K)} and λ_nx by P_n(x)), log(ω/ω^{μ_n}) = O(n^{−(K+1)p}) on both circles, and Δ_k depends analytically on (ρ²)^{±1},
(−ω)^{±1/2}, which are bounded above and below. So Δ_k = Δ_k^{μ_n} + O(n^{−(K+1)p}), and Lemma E3.4(iii). ∎

## 5. Proposition E3.6: small norm with the band factor

**Proposition E3.6 (PROVED-mod-cite: E2 Prop E2.5 (p > 1); E_plt1 Prop E′.5′ (p < 1, contour and super-small jumps)).** For
p ≠ 1, K ≥ 1, define for μ near 0: R⁽¹⁾(μ) := principal parts of Δ₁^μ at 0, 4c; R⁽¹⁾_in(μ) := R⁽¹⁾(μ) − Δ₁^μ (analytic in the
discs); F(μ) := R⁽¹⁾_in(μ)Δ₁^μ + Δ₂^μ; R_1⁽¹⁾(μ) := Σ_eRes_eΔ₁^μ, R_1⁽²⁾(μ) := Σ_eRes_eF(μ). These are entire in μ (Lemma E3.4(iii)),
R_1⁽¹⁾(0) = A₀ + A_r, R_1⁽²⁾(0) = E2's R_1⁽²⁾. Then

  R_1 = R_1⁽¹⁾(μ_n)/n + R_1⁽²⁾(μ_n)/n² + O(n⁻³ + n^{−1−(K+1)p}).

*Proof.* As E2 Prop E2.5, with Δ_k replaced by Δ_k^{μ_n} + O(n^{−(K+1)p}) (Lemma E3.5): the second Neumann iterate gives
𝒞(Δ₁^{μ_n}χ)/n + 𝒞((R⁽¹⁾_in(μ_n)Δ₁^{μ_n} + Δ₂^{μ_n})χ)/n² + O(n⁻³ + n^{−1−(K+1)p}), and the residue evaluation is unchanged since
Δ_k^{μ_n} is meromorphic with the same pole orders (Lemma E3.4(iii)). For p < 1 the jumps off the circles (lips at ±θ_L, the seam
segments with the glue jump, Σ_v, saturated segment, Σ^△, top segment) are super-small exactly as in E_plt1 Prop E′.5′, and the
uniform Cauchy bound is Paper 2 F1 (all p). ∎

## 6. Proofs

**Lemma E3.7 (PROVED; checks/check_e3_mu.py, sympy, all assertions pass).** Let e(μ) be the 1/n coefficient of
√((P₁ + R_1⁽¹⁾(μ)/n)₁₂(P₁ + R_1⁽¹⁾(μ)/n)₂₁)/c minus d₁, i.e. e(μ) = i(A(μ)₂₁ − A(μ)₁₂)/(2c) − d₁ with A(μ) = R_1⁽¹⁾(μ). Then,
modulo monomials of weighted degree ≥ 4 in (μ₁, μ₂, μ₃) (μ₄, … do not enter below weighted degree 4),

  e(μ) = cp(μ₁ + 6cμ₂ + 30c²μ₃) = cp·M_μ(4c).

*Method.* As check_e_plt1_sym.py (which is the case μ = (λ)), with (−ω)^{±1} multiplied by e^{±𝓡M_μ} and ρ² by e^{−𝓡M_μ}
(Lemma E3.4(i)), 𝓡 = z^{1/2}u near 4c (u = (z−4c)^{1/2}) and 𝓡 = −v(4c+v²)^{1/2} on z = −v², M_μ near 4c resp. on z = −v², all

## 8. p = 1 (assessment; GAP)

At p = 1 neither Lemma F.1 nor F.1′ applies (H_n = e^{2j(n/z)} is not flat, E_pgt1 §9), and the x-frame Bessel parametrix fails

(a) *Route A on the lattice s ∈ 3/2 + ℕ₀.* Proposition E1.4(iii) transfers the second-order constant from x^mTC to σ_{3/2+m}, with
the same ε = 1/12 for every m. It needs (R1) and the second-order expansion of b_n, α_n for x^mTC and x^{m+1}TC. E_p1 Lemma E1.5
has closed forms for m ≤ 2; for m ≥ 3 the Darboux step needs g_n(x^{m−1}TC) = −π_{n+1}(0)/π_n(0), a Riccati recursion without
a known closed form, whose asymptotic expansion would have to be justified (Birkhoff–Adams-type theory for linear recurrences with
coefficients expanding in 1/n; not read, so not usable under Rule C).
(b) *Route C at second order.* Requires the Δ₂ data of the three-circle symmetric frame of RH₁(r) (Airy at ±2, the Fisher–Hartwig
point 0 with the alternating phase (−1)^N) and the second-order small norm there — the analogue of E2 in E_p1's frame, including
the alternating parts that E_p1 leaves undetermined (Conjecture 13.1 of Paper II). Conjecture E3.C predicts the answer
d₂(1, s) − 1/12.
