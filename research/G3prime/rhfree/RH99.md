## 0. Notation and elementary identities

- ℰ(u) := ψ(u) − u for u > 0 (renamed from E to avoid a clash with the error integral E; F6), with ψ(u) = 0 for u < 2. Then (3.4) gives |ℰ(u)| ≤ C₀(√u log²(u + 2) + 1) for all u > 0.
- Fix x ≥ 2 and T ≥ 2. Write a_u := a_u(x), and note:
  - a_u is continuous and piecewise a power;
  - |∂_u a_u| ≤ (3/2)a_u/u;
  - a_{u′}/a_u ∈ [e^{−3κ/2}, e^{3κ/2}] for u′ ∈ [u, ue^κ].
- **(0.1) Subtracted term.** For y > 0, x∫_{xe^{−κ}}^{x}a_v(y)dv/v = ∫_y^{ye^κ}a_u(x)du.
  - Substitute v = xy/u. Then a_v(y) = min((x/u)^{1/2}, (u/x)^{3/2}) = a_x(u).
  - Also x·a_x(u)/u = a_u(x): check the cases u ≤ x and u ≥ x.
- **(0.2) Δ.** Since y + y/T = ye^κ, the bracket in L is
  Δ(y) := Σ_{y<n≤ye^κ}Λ(n)a_n − ∫_y^{ye^κ}a_u du = ∫_{(y, ye^κ]}a_u dℰ(u), and L = ∫_0^∞Δ(y)²dy/y.
- **(0.3) Mellin.** ∫_0^∞a_u u^{−it}du = x^{1−it}(1/(3/2 − it) + 1/(½ + it)) = 2x^{1−it}/((½ + it)(3/2 − it)).
  NUMERICAL: [1] of `checks/check_99.out`: 6.5·10⁻¹⁴ and 3.0·10⁻¹³ at t = 0 and 1.7; 7.3·10⁻⁹ at t = 25, where the
  oscillatory quadrature is loose.
  - Hence the bracket in (9.5) is P(t) := ∫_0^∞a_u u^{−it}dℰ(u).
  - This converges absolutely: a_u ≪ u^{−3/2} at ∞ and ≪ u^{1/2} at 0, and |dℰ| ≤ dψ + du.

## 1. Lemma 9.7* (Plancherel form of (9.7))

**Lemma 9.7*.** On RH, for x ≥ 1 and T ≥ 2: L(x, T) = R(x, T) + O((log T)²/T), with an absolute O-constant.
The O-terms of (3.11) are uniform in x ≥ 1 and t. (C8) §3 writes the remaining terms of (3.14) out explicitly; bounding them ourselves gives the uniformity (F7).

*Proof.*
1. **G_κ is a Fourier transform.** Let g(s) := κ^{−1}∫_{(e^{s−κ/2}, e^{s+κ/2}]}a_u dℰ(u) = κ^{−1}Δ(e^{s−κ/2}).
   - By Fubini (absolute integrability, (0.3)) and κ^{−1}∫_{−κ/2}^{κ/2}e^{−itv}dv = sin(κt/2)/(κt/2):
     ∫_ℝ g(s)e^{−its}ds = (sin(κt/2)/(κt/2))·P(t) = G_κ(t).
   - g ∈ L¹ ∩ L²: Δ(y) ≪ a_y(κy log y + √y log²y) as y → ∞, and Δ(y) ≪ κy^{3/2}x^{−1/2} as y → 0.
2. **Plancherel.** ∫_ℝ|G_κ|²dt = 2π∫_ℝ|g|²ds = (2π/κ²)∫_ℝΔ(e^{s−κ/2})²ds = (2π/κ²)L(x, T). This uses (0.1)–(0.2),
   with y = e^{s−κ/2} and ds = dy/y.
3. **Zero side.** By (3.11), P(t) = −2x^{1/2−it}S_x(t) + ε(t), where S_x(t) := Σ_γ x^{iγ}c(t − γ),
   c(u) = 1/(1 + u²), and |ε(t)| ≪ x^{−1/2}log(|t| + 2) for x ≥ 1.
   - On RH the zeros are symmetric under γ ↦ −γ with multiplicity, so S_x(−t) = conj S_x(t).
   - Hence ∫_ℝ φ|S_x|² = 2∫_0^∞φ|S_x|², where φ(t) := (sin(κt/2)/(κt/2))².
   - |S_x(t)| ≤ Σ_γ c(t − γ) ≪ log(|t| + 2), using (C1): N(u + 1) − N(u) ≪ log(|u| + 2).
   - ∫_ℝ φ(t)log²(|t| + 2)dt ≪ κ^{−1}log²(1/κ) ≪ T log²T.
   - Expanding |P|² and using Cauchy–Schwarz on the cross term:
     ∫|G_κ|² = 4x∫_ℝφ|S_x|² + O(x^{1/2}·x^{−1/2}T log²T) + O(x^{−1}T log²T) = 8x∫_0^∞φ|S_x|² + O(T log²T).
4. **Combine.** L = (κ²/2π)∫|G_κ|² = (4xκ²/π)∫_0^∞φ|S_x|² + O(κ²T log²T) = R(x, T) + O(log²T/T), since κ ≤ 1/T. ∎

## 2. Lemma 9.9* (removing the weight; full range)

Put D(y) := ψ(ye^κ) − ψ(y) − δy = ∫_{(y, ye^κ]}dℰ(u), and I(Y, δ) := ∫_1^Y D(y)²dy ((C8) (9.1)).

**Lemma 9.9*.** On RH, for x ≥ 2 and T ≥ 2, with M := 4x³∫_x^∞I(y, δ)y^{−5}dy and E := ∫_0^∞R(y)²dy/y, where
R := Δ − a_y D:

  |L(x, T) − M − δ²/(3x)| ≤ 2√((M + δ²/(3x))·E) + E and the stronger  |√L − √(M + δ²/(3x))| ≤ √E (Minkowski),
  and  E ≤ C κ²x log⁴(x + 2)  (C absolute).

(Rule R F1: Cauchy–Schwarz controls the main term M + δ²/(3x), not M alone.)

*Proof.*
1. **Decomposition.** Δ(y) = a_y D(y) + R(y), with R(y) = ∫_{(y, ye^κ]}(a_u − a_y)dℰ(u).
   - Integrating by parts: R(y) = (a_{ye^κ} − a_y)ℰ(ye^κ) − ∫_y^{ye^κ}ℰ(u)∂_u a_u du.
   - By §0, |R(y)| ≤ 3κe^{3κ/2}a_y·max_{[y,ye^κ]}|ℰ| ≪ κa_y(√y log²(y + 2) + 1).
2. **Bound for E.** a_y² = min(y/x, (x/y)³). So
   E ≪ κ²∫_0^∞a_y²(log⁴(y + 2) + y^{−1})dy ≪ κ²(∫_0^x(y/x)log⁴(y + 2)dy + ∫_x^∞(x/y)³log⁴(y + 2)dy + 4/3)
   ≪ κ²x log⁴(x + 2).
3. **Main term (exact calculus).** Let J(y) := ∫_0^y D². Then
   ∫_0^∞a_y²D²dy/y = x^{−1}J(x) + x³∫_x^∞y^{−4}dJ(y). Integrate the second piece by parts:
   - the boundary term at ∞ vanishes, since J(y) ≪ δ²y³ + y²log⁴y (F6);
   - this gives −J(x)/x + 4x³∫_x^∞J(y)y^{−5}dy.

   Hence ∫_0^∞a_y²D²dy/y = 4x³∫_x^∞J y^{−5}dy.
   - On (0, 1) we have ye^κ < 2, so D(y) = −δy and J(y) = I(y, δ) + δ²/3 for y ≥ 1.
   - So the main term equals M + δ²/(3x).
   - NUMERICAL: [2] of `checks/check_99.out`, agreement to 2·10⁻⁵–8·10⁻⁵ (discretization).
4. **Combine.** L = ∫(a_y D + R)²dy/y. Cauchy–Schwarz on the cross term gives the bound. ∎

**Corollary 9.9′ (full range).** Assume RH and M ≪ xδ log T. Then, uniformly for T ≤ x ≤ T^{M₀} with M₀ fixed:

  L(x, T) = M + O(xδ^{3/2}(log T)^{1/2}log²x + κ²x log⁴x).

The error, divided by xδ log T, is ≪ δ^{1/2}log²x/(log T)^{1/2} → 0.
- This is the conditional form of (C8)'s full-range claim (it uses M ≪ xδ log T, which Goldston does not assume).
  For x ≥ T ≥ e, its first term is smaller than Goldston's stated O(x(log x)⁴/T^{3/2}) by a log power (F4).
- Lemma P1 uses the Minkowski form, which needs no hypothesis on M.

NUMERICAL ([3] of `checks/check_99.out`; actual primes up to 8·10⁶; (x, T) ∈ {(2·10³, 100), …, (10⁵, 3000)}):
- |L − M|/M ranges over 1.7·10⁻⁴ to 4.9·10⁻³.
- It is always below the proved bound (2√(ME) + E)/M, which ranges over 4.4·10⁻⁴ to 1.4·10⁻².
- E/(κ²x log⁴x) ≤ 5·10⁻⁶: the constant is far from sharp.
