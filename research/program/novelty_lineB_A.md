## 1. Christoffel transfer formulas: KNOWN (classical)

**OPENED**: M. Derevyagin, V. Derkach, *Darboux transformations of Jacobi matrices and Padé approximation*, arXiv:1012.3712v2
(LAA 2011). Section 1, verbatim (PDF text):
- "As is known [6], a monic Jacobi matrix J admits the unique LU -factorization J = LU with the bidiagonal matrices L and U
  having the forms [(1.3): L unit lower bidiagonal with subdiagonal l_j, U upper bidiagonal with diagonal u_j and superdiagonal 1]
  if and only if the condition (1.4) Pj (0) ≠ 0, j ∈ Z+ is satisfied. The Christoffel transformation of J is defined as follows
  J = LU → JC := U L. The matrix JC is the tridiagonal matrix associated to the measure tdσ(t) [6]."
- "Notice that the LU -factorization of a monic Jacobi matrix is unique while the U L-factorization depends on the free
  parameter s−1 . Both the Christoffel and Geronimus transformation are also called the Darboux transformations [6]."
- Their [6] is "M.I. Bueno, F. Marcellán, Darboux transformation and perturbation of linear functionals, Linear Algebra Appl.,
  Vol. 384 (2004), 215–242." I SAW this paper but did not open it. It is also cited as [1] in Garza–Hernández–Marcellán (opened,
  uc3m e-archivo 10016/5908), which says canonical spectral transforms on the real line "considered by several authors (see [1],
  [18], [20] and [21]) in connection with bispectral problems and LU, UL, and QR factorization of Jacobi matrices".

The repo's Lemma CT is the LU→UL statement with u_j = g_j and l_j = h_j. CT-Ch and CT₁(i) give the same statement in symmetric
(Cholesky) form. **OPENED**: R. Bailey, M. Derevyagin, *Complex Jacobi matrices generated by Darboux transformations*,
arXiv:2107.09824, §5, Prop. 5.1 (as extracted by the fetch tool). For positive-definite ℒ and κ ∈ ℂ±, J − κI = LLᵀ and
J_C(κ) = LᵀL + κI corresponds to ℒ*. The repo uses κ = 0, which is real and at the bottom of the spectrum, where the Cholesky
factorization exists because J > 0. The repo already labels this "classical, novelty not claimed", and that label is correct.
Suggested citation: Bueno–Marcellán (2004) as the standard reference (needs to be opened before citing), and Derevyagin–Derkach
(2011) §1 (opened). Chihara 1978 Ch. I (kernel polynomials) is SEEN only.

## 2. Lemma CS in chain-sequence form: KNOWN as a statement (2025); older source unconfirmed

**OPENED**: Zejun Dai, Daxiong Piao, Jinglai Qiao, *Construction and Characterization of Oscillatory Chain Sequences*,
arXiv:2508.07653v1 [math.DS], 11 Aug 2025. No journal reference is given. Verbatim:
- Definition: "a sequence {an}n=1∞ qualifies as a chain sequence if it admits the decomposition: [a_n = g_n(1 − g_{n−1})] where
  the parameter sequence {gn}n=1∞ satisfies 0≤gn≤1."
- **Proposition 2.1**: "Suppose lim an = 1/4. If {an} is a chain sequence with a parameter sequence {gn}, then lim gn exists and
  lim gn = 1/2."
- The authors introduce it with "But we can prove following proposition" and give no attribution. Their proof iterates
  f(x) = 1 − 1/(4x), whose iterates converge to ½ on [½, 1] (their Lemma 2.2). This is the same parabolic fixed-point mechanism as
  in the repo's Lemma CS and Lemma D.
- Caveat: I read the proof through a summarizing fetch tool, and its step 5 (δ_{n+1} ≈ δ_n/(1 + 2δ_n)) looked sketchy in that
  summary. Only the statement above is quoted verbatim. The repo's proof is independent and self-contained, so nothing depends on
  their rigor.

Proposition 2.1 covers **every** parameter sequence. Therefore it gives:
- (a) CS: the minimal parameter m_n = h_n/α_n → ½.
- (b) CT₁ "⟸" and Lemma D(b): the factorization J(xμ) = UL with free first parameter. With k_n := g_n/α̃_n one gets
  β̃_n/(α̃_{n−1}α̃_n) = (1 − k_{n−1})k_n, and k_0 ∈ (0, 1) is free, which corresponds to the Geronimus parameter / mass at 0.
  Prop. 2.1 then gives k_n → ½.

The correspondence between chain sequences and [0, ∞) is classical. **OPENED**: K. K. Behera, A. Swaminathan, arXiv:1701.07960,
§3, verbatim: "the true interval of orthogonality is a subset of [0,∞] if and only if b_{n+1}>0 for n≥0 and there are numbers
g_n such that 0≤g_0<1, 0<g_n<1, n≥1, satisfying (1−g_{n−1})g_n = a_n²/(b_n b_{n+1}) = ω_n(0), n≥1". The paper cites Ismail,
*Classical and Quantum OPs in One Variable*, Cor. 7.2.4 (SEEN only), and defines "The minimal parameter sequence
{m_n}_{n=0}^∞ of the positive chain sequence {d_n} is defined as m_0=0, … 0<m_n<1, with d_n=(1−m_{n−1})m_n". It also states
"It is known that m_k≤g_k≤M_k, k≥0, for any parameter sequence". These are exactly the repo's a_n = β_n/(α_{n−1}α_n) and
m_n = h_n/α_n.

**OPENED** for background: R. Szwarc, *Chain sequences, orthogonal polynomials, and Jacobi matrices*, J. Approx. Theory 92
(1998) 59–73, doi:10.1006/jath.1996.3109. Abstract: "Chain sequences are positive sequences [a n ] of the form
a n = g n(1− g n−1 ) for a nonnegative sequence [ g n ]. This concept was introduced by Wall in connection with continued
fractions." The paper is about the a_n ≥ ¼ regime (Chihara's conjecture, Chihara 1978 Thm III.5.8 and Ex. III.5.6). It has no
limit-of-parameters theorem.

**Not confirmed**: whether Wall (1948) or Chihara (1978, Ch. III) states "a_n → ¼ ⇒ every parameter → ½". Neither book was
accessible. Chihara, Trans. AMS 104 (1962), "Chain sequences and orthogonal polynomials", returned HTTP 403 (SEEN only). The way
Dai–Piao–Qiao present Prop. 2.1 ("we can prove", no citation) is weak evidence that the statement is not a standard textbook
item. The convergent case a_n → a < ¼ (minimal parameter → (1 − √(1 − 4a))/2) is folklore-level, but I did not find it in an
opened source.

**Verdict: KNOWN** (statement in arXiv:2508.07653, Prop. 2.1). Recommended relabel: "PROVED (self-contained); KNOWN in
chain-sequence form: Dai–Piao–Qiao arXiv:2508.07653 Prop. 2.1 (any parameter sequence); classical origin (Wall/Chihara)
unconfirmed".

## 3. Theorem SH and the CT₁ converse: PARTIALLY KNOWN

**Derivation.** Combine the classical correspondence (§1, §2: supp ⊂ [0, ∞) ⇔ chain sequence; Christoffel = LU→UL) with Prop. 2.1:
- Law P at any scale s_n with s_{n−1}/s_n → 1 gives a_n = β_n/(α_{n−1}α_n) → ¼.
- Prop. 2.1 then gives m_n → ½, hence s_ng_n, s_nh_n → c.
- Lemma CT then gives Law P for xσ.
- In reverse, any parameter sequence → ½ gives CT₁ "⟸".

So Theorem SH without a rate, and CT₁ "⟺", are a few lines from published statements. A specialist would regard them as easy
consequences.

**Closest prior work for invariance statements (OPENED).** Bailey–Derevyagin, arXiv:2107.09824 (fetch-tool extraction; the quote
fragments are verbatim):
- Prop. 3.3: if J_m is in 𝒩(a,c), "the Christoffel transformation J_m* of J_m at κ∈ℂ∖ℝ is also in the same Nevai class 𝒩(a,c)."
- Prop. 4.4 is the Geronimus analogue (κ ∈ ℂ∖ℝ, "provided the latter exists").
- Section 3: "such properties do not hold in general". Remark 3.4 (Stahl): "the Christoffel transformation of the Chebyshev
  polynomials at some real points leads to an unbounded Jacobi matrix."
- What differs: their setting is bounded Nevai classes with non-real κ. The repo's case has real κ = 0 at the endpoint of the
  support, decaying coefficients (a compact Jacobi operator with 0 as the only accumulation point), and rescaled limits. This
  bounded-case paper does not cover it.

**Searched and not found.** I found no statement of "s_nα_n → 2c, s_n√β_n → c is invariant under x·dσ" for decaying or for
unbounded (Laguerre-type) regularly varying coefficients. Places checked:
- Van Assche, *Compact Jacobi matrices: from Stieltjes to Krein and M(a,b)*, arXiv:math/9510214 (OPENED; covers compactness,
  Krein, M(a,b); no Christoffel or rescaled-limit invariance).
- Nevai–Van Assche, *Compact perturbations of orthogonal polynomials*, Pacific J. Math. 153 (1992) 163–184 (OPENED; relative
  asymptotics against a comparison system; no Christoffel step).
- Szwarc 1998 (OPENED).
- SEEN only, not opened:
  - Geronimo–Van Assche, *Relative asymptotics for orthogonal polynomials with unbounded recurrence coefficients*, J. Approx.
    Theory 62 (1990) 47–69, doi:10.1016/0021-9045(90)90046-S. Metadata only, via Crossref; the publisher page returned 403. This
    is the most likely place for a regularly-varying analogue and should be checked by hand.
  - Szwarc, *Chain sequences and compact perturbations of orthogonal polynomials*, Math. Z. 217 (1994) 57–71. A likely place for
    chain-sequence + compact-perturbation arguments; must be read.
  - Simon, *Szegő's Theorem and Its Descendants* (2011). Nevai 1979 Memoir. Máté–Nevai–Totik. Zhedanov, rational spectral
    transformations (JCAM 1997). Uvarov 1969.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** The ingredients are known (chain-sequence correspondence, Prop. 2.1, Christoffel formulas), so the
no-rate SH and CT₁ "⟺" are an immediate corollary of published statements. I found no explicit published statement in the
decaying/rescaled ("Law P") setting. The repo's node-agnostic, scale-agnostic formulation (any s_n with s_{n−1}/s_n → 1) appears
unrecorded, but should be presented as a corollary, not as a new theorem.

## 4. Rate transfer SH-r / CS-r: NOT FOUND

I found no source on rates of convergence of chain-sequence parameters near ¼. In particular, nothing covers O(√r_n) for the
parameters, O(r_n) for their increments, the exact identity n^p√β̃_n = n^p√β_n κ_n^{1/2}(G_n/G_{n−1})^{1/2}, or the "only
increments enter" mechanism. Szwarc (1998, opened) gives sharp *sum* estimates Σ(a_n − ¼) ≤ ¼ and Σ_{n≥N}(a_n − ¼) ≤ 1/(4N) for
a_n ≥ ¼, which is a different regime. Dai–Piao–Qiao give no rate. The repo's sharpness example (G_n = c + a/√n) also appears new.

**Verdict: NOT FOUND.** This is the part with the best novelty claim. Even so it is elementary, and it should be checked against
Szwarc 1994 (Math. Z.) and Geronimo–Van Assche 1990 before any claim.
